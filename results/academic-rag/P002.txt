On-Device Low-Frequency Mechanical Fault
Detection of Induction Motors using MEMS
Sensors and Edge AI
Vedanta Banerjee, Debjyoti Chowdhury, Madhurima Chattopadhyay
Department of Applied Electronics and Instrumentation Engineering
Heritage Institute of Technology, Kolkata, India
vedantabanerjee01@gmail.com, {debjyoti.chowdhury, madhurima.chattopadhyay}@heritageit.edu
Abstract—This paper presents a TinyML system for end-
to-end, on-device vibration-based mechanical fault detection in
single-phase induction motors. The sensing node integrates a
low-cost MEMS inertial sensor (LSM6DSO) with an ESP32-C3
microcontroller. The proposed firmware implements orientation-
invariant preprocessing, contiguous leakage-safe segmentation,
and int8 quantized inference of a compact multi-channel 1D
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). Window-level outputs are
aggregated using a confidence-weighted majority voting rule to
provide robust diagnoses across four states: motorOFF, mo-
torON, motorON NoFan, and motorON BadFan. By employing
strictly contiguous file splits to prevent temporal data leakage, we
report a window-level macro F1-score of 0.98 and a recording-
level macro F1-score of 0.95 on held-out, position-specific data.
The quantized model requires only 80 KB of Flash and 120 KB
of RAM, with an inference latency of 82 ms. This demonstrates
the feasibility of deploying sophisticated diagnostic AI on low-
power edge devices for real-time industrial condition monitoring
of induction motors.
Index Terms—Condition monitoring, convolutional neural net-
works (CNN), edge AI, induction motors, microcontrollers, pre-
dictive maintenance, TinyML, vibration analysis.
I. INTRODUCTION
Induction motors (IMs) are the industrial backbone, pow-
ering critical infrastructure from HVAC systems to process
conveyors [1]. Given their continuous operation in harsh
environments, reliable performance is a primary driver of
plant availability and energy efficiency. Even marginal im-
provements in early fault detection can significantly reduce
downtime, repair costs, and safety risks [2]. While indus-
trial monitoring often prioritizes bearing failures, this study
focuses on low-frequency mechanical anomalies, specifically
fan unbalance and removal. These faults represent critical
failure modes that lead to motor overheating and accelerated
insulation degradation.
A. Induction Motor Fault Modalities
IM faults are broadly categorized as electrical (e.g., winding
anomalies, broken bars) or mechanical (e.g., bearing defects,
misalignment, and fan damage). Electrical faults are typi-
cally diagnosed through current-signature analysis and time-
frequency processing of stator currents [3], [4]. Conversely,
mechanical faults manifest as localized vibration signatures
requiring accelerometer-based envelope or spectral analysis
[5]. Combining these modalities often enhances diagnostic
breadth and reliability across varied operating conditions [6].
B. Vibration-Based Diagnostics and Edge Computing
Vibration sensing captures the mechanical energy imparted
to motor housings, making it ideal for detecting the impulsive
or modulated patterns characteristic of shaft misalignment
and fan-related anomalies. While classical time-domain de-
scriptors (RMS, kurtosis) and frequency-domain peaks of-
fer interpretability, advanced time-frequency techniques like
STFT or wavelets are essential for non-stationary conditions.
Recent advancements in MEMS accelerometers facilitate cost-
effective, distributed monitoring networks when integrated
with energy-efficient edge nodes [7], enabling sophisticated
diagnostics without reliance on cloud-based processing.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Review of Literature and Prior Art
Induction motor fault diagnosis has evolved from canonical
signal processing to sophisticated machine learning. Estab-
lished surveys emphasize that robust condition monitoring
requires specialized preprocessing for non-stationary signals
and careful consideration of mechanical coupling effects [1].
Classical methodologies typically utilize engineered time-
and frequency-domain features paired with SVMs or tree-
based classifiers to distinguish mechanical faults in controlled
environments [5]. Comparative studies indicate that while
current-signature analysis is effective for electromechanical
interactions, vibration-based sensing remains more sensitive
to purely mechanical anomalies [6].
Recent advancements favor end-to-end deep learning, where
CNNs trained on time-frequency representations achieve high
diagnostic accuracy, particularly on standard bearing datasets
[8], [9]. These successes motivate the shift toward TinyML,
where models are quantized for microcontroller deployment.
However, existing literature reveals critical methodological
gaps: (i) a lack of reproducible metrics for full TinyML
stacks (Flash, RAM, and latency), (ii) insufficient exploration
of mounting-position variability and orientation-invariant pre-
processing, (iii) a lack of real-time synchronization between
hardware acquisition and inference, and (iv) prevalent data

The end-to-end architecture is illustrated in the block dia-

A. Hardware Architecture and Sensor Placement
The sensing node (Fig. 2) is designed for high-fidelity
vibration acquisition within a constrained power budget. It
integrates an LSM6DSO 6-DoF IMU with a Seeed Studio
XIAO ESP32-C3, featuring a 160 MHz RISC-V core and
400 KB SRAM. The tri-axial accelerometer is configured to
a full-scale range of ±4g, providing sufficient dynamic range
for industrial motor vibrations. To optimize battery longevity
while ensuring signal integrity for low-frequency faults, data
is sampled at fs = 445 Hz. This configuration directly aligns
with the IMU’s internal Output Data Rate (ODR) to prevent
aliasing and satisfies the Nyquist criterion for mechanical
anomalies manifesting below 220 Hz.

B. Windowing and Data Augmentation
The continuous data stream is partitioned into temporal win-
dows using a sliding window approach. We define a window
length L = 512 samples and a stride S = 256, resulting
in a 50% overlap to preserve transient features at segment
boundaries. Each input instance X ∈R512×4 represents the
four channels (ˆax, ˆay, ˆaz, av).
During the training phase, on-the-fly augmentations are
applied to improve model generalization:
• Additive Gaussian Noise: X ′ = X + N(0, σnoise),
where σnoise is 1% of the signal’s standard deviation.
• Random Amplitude Scaling: X ′ = X · α, where α ∼
U(0.9, 1.1).
Augmentations were disabled during validation and held-out
testing to ensure a deterministic assessment of base model
performance.
V. MODEL DESIGN AND TRAINING
A. Architecture
The model is a compact multi-channel 1D CNN ar-
chitecture (implemented using Conv2D layers with N × 1
kernels) optimized for low parameter count and quantization
friendliness. It utilizes elongated temporal kernels to capture
waveform envelopes, small channel counts, batch normaliza-
tion, and dropout for regularization. The feature extractor is
followed by global average pooling and a small dense head.
Total trainable parameters are approximately 15k. The layer
configurations in Table II correspond to the input window size
of L = 512.
B. Training recipe
Optimization uses Adam (initial LR = 1 × 10−3), batch
size 32, sparse categorical cross-entropy, ReduceLROnPlateau
(patience 8), and EarlyStopping (patience 12). Representative
calibration windows (1k–5k, uniformly sampled across mount
positions and classes) are used for post-training integer quan-
tization (PTQ).
VI. ON-DEVICE INTEGRATION AND RESOURCE PROFILE
A. Quantization and TFLM integration
We apply full integer PTQ (int8) and integrate the ‘.tflite‘
model into TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers (TFLM). The
firmware embeds StandardScaler constants and uses integer
arithmetic consistent with host quantization parameters. The
TFLM build includes only the operators required by the model
to minimize firmware size.

VII. EVALUATION PROTOCOL
A. Leakage-safe splits
Train/Validation/Test splits are contiguous by file to prevent
any window overlap across splits. The final generalization is
measured on the 12 held-out files in datasets/test/ (3
positions × 4 classes).
B. Metrics and Aggregation
We report window-level metrics (Precision, Recall, F1) and
recording-level metrics aggregated via confidence-weighted
majority voting. For each held-out recording, for each class
k, we compute:
scorek = (mean confidencek) × (countk).
The class with the maximum scorek is assigned.

IX. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. Performance and Resource Utilization
The proposed pipeline achieves high diagnostic accuracy
within strict embedded constraints. Validation results yield
a window-level macro F1-score of 0.98 (Table III), while
recording-level performance on 12 held-out, position-specific
files reaches 0.95 (Table IV). This confirms that confidence-
weighted aggregation effectively mitigates transient window
misclassifications. On-device metrics for the int8 quantized
model include 80 KB Flash, 120 KB peak RAM, and a
mean inference latency of 82 ± 7 ms. Given the sampling
stride Tstride ≈575 ms, the system consumes only 14.2% of
the available temporal budget, leaving significant headroom
for auxiliary tasks or low-power sleep states.
B. Power Profiling and Error Analysis
Energy efficiency is critical for battery-powered edge nodes.
With the ESP32-C3 consuming ∼25 mA at 3.3 V during
active inference, the energy cost is approximately 6.77 mJ per
inference. Utilizing a 1500 mAh battery, the node can theo-
retically sustain continuous operation for 14.1 days, perform-
ing over 2.1 million inferences. Error analysis indicates that
most misclassifications occur within the motorON BadFan
class due to subtle vibrational similarities with normal run-
ning states. However, the orientation-invariant magnitude and
elongated temporal kernels significantly enhance robustness
against mounting variability and transient environmental noise.
C. Comparative Evaluation
The proposed multi-channel 1D CNN was evaluated against
three baselines using the same leakage-safe protocol. The
model (0.95 F1) outperformed linear SVMs (0.84), Random
Forests (0.86), and parameter-matched conventional 1D-CNNs
(0.92). These results validate the efficacy of the magnitude-
integrated feature extraction and the post-inference aggrega-
tion strategy.
D. Limitations and Industrial Outlook
Unlike many accuracy-centric studies, this work enforces
contiguous file splits to eliminate temporal leakage and
provides empirical TinyML deployment metrics. A primary
limitation is the specific focus on fan-related faults in a single
motor frame (0.37 kW). While fan unbalance is a critical

X. CONCLUSION
This study presents a comprehensive TinyML framework
for on-device induction motor fault detection using a low-cost
MEMS accelerometer and an ESP32-C3 microcontroller. By
enforcing strictly contiguous, leakage-safe data splits, the
system ensures methodological rigor and robust generalization,
achieving a window-level macro F1 of 0.98 and a recording-
level F1 of 0.95. The solution is highly resource-efficient,
requiring only 80 KB of Flash and 120 KB of RAM. With
an inference latency of 82 ms and an estimated energy
consumption of 6.77 mJ per cycle, the system can operate
continuously for approximately 14 days on a 1500 mAh
battery. This demonstrates that high-fidelity mechanical diag-
nostics can be localized on resource-constrained hardware with
high energy-determinism. While this work focuses on fan-
related anomalies, the orientation-invariant pipeline provides a
scalable blueprint for broader industrial maintenance. Future
research will expand the fault spectrum to include bearing
defects and misalignment to further advance the versatility of
Edge AI in Industry 4.0 environments.
