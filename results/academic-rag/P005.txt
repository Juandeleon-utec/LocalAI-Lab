Abstract—In today’s industrial world, it is essential to ensure 
efficient and safe operation of machines to ensure productivity 
and reduce costs. Vibration analysis is one of the most effective 
tools used for this purpose. Vibration analysis is used to monitor 
the condition of rotating machines, detect potential problems 
early and avoid costly breakdowns. In this article, we will 
explore how the integration of vibration analysis with fog 
computing and edge artificial intelligence (Edge AI) can create 
a powerful system for real-time machine monitoring and 
predictive maintenance. Keywords—predictive maintenance, 
FOG computing, Edge AI, rotating machine 
I. 
INTRODUCTION 
Management of resources in industrial computers demands 
strategic decision-making. 
Resource constraints, heterogeneity, the dynamic and 
diversified nature of workloads, and the variability of FOG or 
Edge computing systems have made managing resources 
even more difficult to solve in the FOG landscape. 
 
Recently, technologies based on artificial intelligence (AI) & 
machine learning (ML) have been implemented to tackle this 
issue. AI & ML systems that can make sequential judgments, 
such as reinforcement learning, appear to be the most 
promising for solving such challenges. However, these 
algorithms provide unique issues such as high variance, 
interpretability and online training. The dynamics of the ever-
changing FOG/EDGE environment necessitate systems that 
learn online and adapt to an evolving computing environment 
[1]. 
II. 
MATERIALS AND METHODS 
Integrating vibration analysis with fog computing and edge 
AI can create a powerful system for real-time machine 
tracking and maintenance prediction. Here’s how these 
concepts can be integrated [2]: 
A. Data acquisition and collection [3]: 
• Sensors (e.g. those adapted to the context of vibration, 
gyroscopes, accelerometers, speed sensors) are 
attached to the machines so as to collect the maximum 
amount of data on vibrations for greater reliability. 
• These sensors continuously measure vibrations to have 
a good real-time approach. 
B. Edge Processing (Edge AI – Edge Computing) [4] :  
• The raw vibration data is sent to nearby edge devices 
(e.g., industrial gateways or small computers) to 
improve communication performance. 
• These edge devices perform initial processing and 
analysis using embedded AI algorithms. 
• Edge AI can perform tasks like:  
o Filtering out noise (Kalman Filter). 
o Extracting relevant features from the vibration 
signals 
o Running fast Fourier transforms (FFT) to analyse 
frequency components in the frequential domain. 
C. Fog Layer [5] :  
• The fog computing layer sits between edge devices and 
the cloud. thus, reducing network traffic, and avoiding 
network bottlenecks between devices and the cloud. 
• It consists of local servers or micro data centers that 
can aggregate data from multiple edge devices. 
• In this layer, more complex analysis can be performed, 
such as:  
o Comparing vibration patterns across multiple 
machines 
o Running more sophisticated machine learning 
models 
o Detecting anomalies that may indicate potential 
failures 
D. Cloud Integration [6]:  
• While most processing occurs at the edge and fog 
layers, the cloud can be used for :

III. 
BACKGROUND 
The aim of our study is to create a novel automated 
framework capable of predicting and diagnosing incipient 
failures of rotating machinery using Edge AI. Our model has 
as inputs vibration signals from an accelerometer-gyroscope 
and has as outputs an estimation of the rotating machine 
condition as well as the degree of wear and fault diagnosis of 
the components of these machines. 
First, the signals are gathered and analyzed to identify 
probable fault signatures. The generated signals are then 
analyzed to identify features associated to gearbox component 
defects at specified frequencies. Finally, these attributes are 
employed as inputs in pattern recognition models to assess 
whether a failure exists, in which component, and at what 
stage it happens.

A. Noise analysis 
Acquired signals often contain background noise from the 
environment. The most commonly used filters were Kalman 
filters [13], and extended Kalman filters [14] [15], but it turned 
out that for rotating machines TQWT techniques are more 
effective for denoising vibration signals from bearings and 
rotating machines [16].. 
B. Vibration signature assessment 
Induction machines in industry are defined by their signal 
processing method and measured using a machine vibration 
analysis device. ISO 2372 (108) uses the Root Mean Square 
(RMS) velocity of the machine housing as the criterion. 
According to NASA, the RMS velocity ranges from 10 to 
1000 Hz. The resulting value is subject to standard limits 
[17]. 
C. The Fourier-based Synchrosqueezing Transform 
The SST has two goals: to give a concentrated 
representation of multi-component signals in the TF plane, as 
well as a decomposition method for separating and 
demodulating the various modes. A theoretical result, initially 
stated in [18] in the CWT context, demonstrates its utility for 
well-separated low-modulated multicomponent signals. This 
section describes STFT-based synchrosqueezing (FSST) and 
concludes the approximation result of [18] to that framework. 
We will pay special emphasis to the differences between 
FSST and CWT-based synchrosqueezing (WSST) [19].

D. AI-based fog/edge computing [1] 
As IoT and machine learning usage grows, cloud and edge 
workloads become more diversified and dynamic. To 
increase human quality of life by combining fog with AI, 
smart resource management is necessary. Traditional cloud 
computing platforms rely on heuristic ways to manage 
resources, failing to account for diverse and dynamic 
workloads (20). Many of these strategies, such as the 
threshold-based strategy, are static heuristics set up offline 
for specific workload scenarios. They cannot grow 
applications dynamically based on workload patterns [21]. 
Scaling up can significantly reduce the performance of 
heuristic approaches. Resource contention is a significant 
issue in fog environments, where co-located applications 
fight for shared resources, leading to poor performance and 
SLA violations [22]. The transition from monolithic to micro-
services and serverless architectures has led to greater 
complexity [23]. Dependency in micro-services can lead to 
SLO breaches due to increased communication costs and 
resource demands in fog computing.

IV. 
THE ARCHITECTURE OF OUR FRAMEWORK 
In this paper, the proposed framework is a new architecture, 
which brings together the elements of FOG Computing and 
also Edge Computing that are at the base of a new predictive 
maintenance system for rotating machines, linking it with the 
tasks required during its development. The complete lifecycle 
of the framework is illustrated in Figure 3 and consists of 
several steps. A data preparation process is applied on the raw 
time-stamped vibration data in order to generate the 
equivalent rotation patterns, which are then denoised in a 
special sub-layer of our FOG. Then, a vibration signature 
extraction procedure is activated, followed by an equivalent 
failure recognition step. The final step of the process takes 
place at the Cloud level, and which allows to store the 
obtained results, and also to feed the training step of the 
learning machines.

REALISATION 
Here are some key points to remember: 
• 
The Jetson Nano enables powerful local processing, 
reducing latency and data transmission costs. 
• 
Real-time analytics at the edge enable rapid detection 
of critical anomalies. 
• 
The cloud provides additional storage and compute 
capacity for deeper analytics and continuous model 
training. 
• 
The architecture is adaptable to numerous types of 
rotating machinery. 
• 
The system can easily scale by adding more Jetson 
Nanos to monitor more machines. 
This framework could significantly improve the reliability of

be particularly useful in manufacturing, energy, or 
transportation industries. 
VII. CONCLUSION 
The proposed framework, because of how it integrates FOG 
Computing and Edge AI, greatly improves the reliability of 
predictive 
maintenance 
for 
rotating 
machines. 
The 
methodology shows a substantial improvement to fault 
detection accuracy and timeliness by leveraging Edge AI's 
real-time processing and FOG nodes' proximity. 
 
In particular, the distributed intelligence at the edge does 
reduce data latency in addition to bandwidth consumption. 
Consequently, this acumen allows faster anomaly detection. 
Edge AI models process sensor data locally, which minimizes 
reliance upon centralized cloud infrastructure. This increases 
system resilience also reduces the impact of network 
connectivity issues. Also, the framework is able to learn on 
the device continuously and this lets predictive adaptive 
models change with the operational traits of the machine so a 
stronger more accurate strategy results. 
 
The article presents empirical results that validate the 
superior performance of this methodology as it can reduce 
costly downtime to optimize maintenance schedules 
regarding rotating machinery all across various industrial 
sectors.
