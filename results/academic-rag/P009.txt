An Intelligent Anomaly Detection Method for
Rotating Machinery Based on
Vibration Vectors
Di Hu, Chen Zhang, Tao Yang
, and Gang Chen
Abstract—Anomaly detection is a common technology for
condition monitoring that is important for safe and reliable
production in industry. In this work, we proposed an anomaly
detection method for rotating machinery based on vibration
vectors, which was inspired by Polar plot in vibration analysis.
The vibration vector, consisting of amplitude and phase, was
selected as monitored indicator at different characteristic
frequencies (CFs). The original vibration vectors obtained by
fast Fourier transform-based order analysis (FFT-OA) were
mapped from Polar to Cartesian plot for simpliﬁed calculation
and better visualization. The acceptance region deﬁned to
distinguish anomalies from normal conditions was learned
by support vector data description (SVDD) model with normal
vibration samples. We proposed the anomaly score to indicate the degree of deviation from normal condition, and the
ﬁt degree to reduce underﬁtting of data description model. Two datasets, obtained from a real steam turbine and a
real steam feed pump respectively, were used to validate the method under constant and variable speed operation
conditions. Compared to the amplitude-based methods, the proposed method exhibited an extremely strong ability to
detect anomalies in advance. Moreover, SVDD performed better than the minimum covariance determinant (MCD) and
isolated forest (iForest) methods in terms of describing the acceptance region in the present work. With low time-
consuming calculation, the acceptance region can be self-evolving easily and has a prospect to the edge computing
for machine condition monitoring.
Index Terms— Condition monitoring, anomaly detection, rotating machinery, vibration signal, vibration vector, support
vector data description.

I. INTRODUCTION
R
OTATING machinery is widely used in power plant,
such as steam turbine, induced draft fan, and circulating
pump, the safe and reliable operation of which is vital for
production beneﬁt [1], [2]. Hence, condition monitoring has
always been an important part of the lifecycle management of
rotating machinery and remains a focus of research. In recent
years, anomaly detection as a hot technology in condition
monitoring has emerged as a new trend, which has been
served to transform traditional corrective maintenance into
condition-based maintenance strategy [3]–[8]. Hawkins [9]
deﬁned an anomaly as an observation generated by a different
mechanism that deviates signiﬁcantly from other observations,
and anomalies can be detected by an acceptance region learned
from normal observations. Vibration monitoring is the most
reliable method for assessing the overall health and condition
changes of a rotor system [10]. Thus, vibration signal-based
anomaly detection is studied with two key issues. One of the
issues is to extract concise and effective features from raw
vibration signals, and the other is to deﬁne the acceptance

In general, the condition monitoring process is divided into
three steps: (1) feature extraction, (2) feature selection, and (3)
classiﬁer establishment. Several feature extraction methods
have been proposed to explore the useful information from
vibration signals, which can be divided into time domain
analysis [11]–[20], frequency domain analysis [21]–[26],
and time–frequency domain analysis [27]–[35]. Artiﬁcial
neural network (ANN) methods have been introduced in
recent years [36]–[40]. The feature selection is related to
classiﬁer and follows the principle of conciseness and effec-
tiveness. Ge et al. [13] proposed a dynamic statistical
approach to detect faults in non-Gaussian vibration systems.
Purarjomandlangrudi et al. [18] used kurtosis and the non-
Gaussianity score as features, and a Gaussian distribution-
based classiﬁer was adopted. Igba et al. [19] used the root
mean square (RMS) and peak values of vibration signals as
monitored indicators for early detection based on the extreme
value theory. Su et al. [41] adopted the short-time Fourier
transform (STFT) to obtain amplitudes at different frequencies;
the classiﬁer was established with an ANN. Tax et al. [42] used
the RMS, kurtosis, and crest-factor as features, and the support
vector data description (SVDD) was used to learn the bound-
ary between normal and abnormal signals. Jiang et al. [43]
used RMS as a vibration feature and established an SVDD-
based classiﬁer for a dual-rotor machine. Sadooghi et al. [44]
performed dimensionality reduction with statistical indicators
which were obtained from time and time–frequency domain,
an SVDD-based classiﬁer was used. In the present study, many
schemes of fault diagnosis classiﬁers have been proposed
to diagnose the fault directly. The fault diagnosis classiﬁer
is a multi-classiﬁer owing to the various fault types, which
the learning process requires both normal and various fault
samples. However, rotating machinery operates under normal
conditions in the most of running time and has various fault
types and rare fault instances, which makes multi-classiﬁer
difﬁcult to train in some situations. The anomaly detection
classiﬁer is a single-classiﬁer and sends abnormal alarms in
the early stage of fault (without knowing the type), and the
learning process only requires normal samples. The arisen
abnormal alarms remind the operator to take intervention
measures to ensure safe and reliable running of machines.
Thus, anomaly detection classiﬁer can provide an acceptance
region to detect anomalies in time and has potential in the
ﬁeld of condition monitoring for major machines.
The widely used anomaly detection classiﬁer in actual pro-
duction is mainly based on ﬁxed threshold [45] of monitored
indicators such as amplitude. Fixed threshold, set artiﬁcially
based on experience, has an obvious shortcoming which causes
either frequent alarms with a low threshold or missing of
best maintenance opportunity with a high threshold. The
vibration vector, consisting of amplitude and phase, contains
comprehensive information about vibration component at char-
acteristic frequencies (CFs) and is a concise and effective
monitored indicator. The Polar plot is a typical vibration data
presentation method [10], on which a vibration vector at any
point in time is plotted as a single point. Bently [46] proposed
a method based on Polar plot to detect early rotor cracks.
A few studies [47]–[50] introduce vibration vector into con-
dition monitoring for rotating machinery, in which the accep-
tance region on the Polar plot is set by experience or not
deﬁned. The anomaly is simply judged by the boundary with
ignoring the degree of deviation from the region, which makes
the approach lack robustness in industrial applications. Mean-
while, in the lifecycle of machine, the location of vibration
vector may change in normal conditions, which requires the
method has self-evolving ability. Consequently, the ﬂexible
and automatic description of acceptance region deserves a
further study.
In this study, we adopt fast Fourier transform-based order
analysis (FFT-OA) to obtain vibration vectors from raw vibra-
tion signal and use SVDD model to learn the acceptance region
at different CFs. The phase information is often ignored in
on-line monitoring, which may miss some signs about early
fault. The amplitude and phase are jointly analyzed in a vibra-
tion vector which is mapped from Polar to Cartesian plot for
simpliﬁed calculation and better visualization. We proposed
the anomaly score to indicate the degree of deviation from the
acceptance region, when SVDD model with Gaussian kernel
function was used. Meanwhile, we proposed ﬁt degree to solve
the underﬁtting problem in the ﬁeld of single-classiﬁer. The
other two methods minimum covariance determinant (MCD)
and isolation forest (iForest) were taken for comparation. The
possible anomaly can be detected when the vibration vectors
are out of acceptance region with high anomaly score. Two
datasets, obtained from a real steam turbine and a real steam
feed pump respectively, are used to validate the method under
constant speed operation (CSO) and variable speed opera-
tion (VSO) conditions. The dataset contains vibration signals
of bearing, which is obtained by synchronous integrated period
sampling. The case studies show that the proposed method
performs better than amplitude-based method in early stage
anomaly detection. The study tries to contribute in follows:
1. The practice dataset shows that acceptance region can
distinguish the abnormalities from normal conditions,
and the acceptance region is learned through intelligent
data description methods rather than human experience.
2. The anomaly score is proposed to express the degree of
deviation from the acceptance region, which provides a
robustness monitoring in industrial applications.
3. The ﬁt degree is deﬁned to indicate the underﬁtting
problem in single-classiﬁer, which ensures the low rate
of missing alarm in monitoring task.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section II
presents the intelligent anomaly detection method for rotating
machinery in detail. In Section III, the actual case studies
validate the proposed method. Conclusions and future work
are presented in Section IV.

D. Proposed Anomaly Detection Method
The vibration monitoring is a common technology for
condition monitoring that is of great signiﬁcance for safe
and reliable running of rotating machinery. However, the
ﬁxed threshold-based methods for monitored indicators such
as amplitude, are applied in on-line monitoring of real pro-
duction, in which thresholds are man-made according to
experience.

The ﬁxed threshold has an obvious shortcoming which
causes either frequent alarms with a low threshold or missing
of best maintenance opportunity with a high threshold. The tra-
ditional vibration monitoring often ignores the phase changes
at different CF. When the rotating machinery is in the normal
operation state, we have found that the 1X vibration vector
is distributed in a certain region in the polar plot, and the
vibration vector with fault deviates from this region. Based on
this motivation, an intelligent and automatic anomaly detection
method that considers the amplitude and phase information
jointly is proposed for fault early warning. The method is
divided into six steps, as shown in Fig. 5.
Step 1: Vibration data acquisition. The proposed method
utilises the vibration data from the condition monitoring
process for rotating machinery. The obtained dataset is divided
into ofﬂine and online dataset. Ofﬂine dataset is used to learn
the acceptance region of the vibration vector for each CF, and
online dataset is used to evaluate the equipment status in real-
time. The data acquisition meets the FFT processing requires.
Step 2: Original vibration signal. The recorded vibration
signal data is pre-processed, including noise reduction and
smoothing, to obtain the original vibration waveform. Each
waveform is a sample that contains the current vibration
information of the target equipment.
Step 3: Vibration vectors. FFT-OA technology is used to
extract information from the vibration waveform, and a series
of vibration vectors corresponding to CFs (0.5X, 1X, 2X…)
are obtained. The vibration vectors contain the amplitude and
phase information and gather in a certain region when the
equipment in normal operation.
Step 4: Learned acceptance region. The amplitude and phase
have different numerical characteristic which amplitude range
is greater than zero whereas the phase varies from 0 to 2π.

Steps 2–3 to assessment whether the vibration vector is within
the acceptance region or not for each CF. In actual production,
the anomaly threshold can be set by anomaly score which
corresponds to anomaly conﬁdence, the higher score the higher
conﬁdence for anomaly alarm. The alarm result is conﬁrmed
by the operation and maintenance personnel. If the alarm is
true, the maintenance activities shall be arranged. If the alarm
is wrong, the SVDD model shall be updated.
In this method, the amplitude and phase of each CF are
considered jointly for the vibration signal. From the exten-
sive historical vibration signal data, the acceptance region of
vibration vector can be learned for each CF, which provides
the evaluation basis for anomaly detection. Compared with the
early warning method based on ﬁxed amplitude threshold, the
proposed method has three advantages: First, it can make full
use of the information from the vibration signal data, including
amplitude and phase. Second, the anomaly determination
criteria come from the description of normal data rather than
human experience. Third, this method can be extended to
any N-order CF, so that can provide a more comprehensive
vibration-based condition monitoring for the machinery.

IV. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
This work proposes an intelligent anomaly detection method
for rotating machinery based on vibration vector in target
characteristic frequency, which is veriﬁed by two practice
case and has a prospect to the edge computing for machine
condition monitoring. The vibration vector joints the amplitude
and phase information, can provide more effective anomaly
detection than amplitude-based method in sometimes. For the
single-classiﬁer training process, the K-fold cross-validation
was applied to avoid overﬁtting, and the ﬁt degree was
proposed and deﬁned to indicate underﬁtting. There is a trade-
off between mean accuracy and ﬁt degree, to obtain a robust
data description model in industry application. The minimum
covariance determinant tends to learn an elliptical region, and
the ﬁt degree is low when the target class does not obey
the Gaussian distribution. The learned acceptance by isolated
forests is unstable, which is related to the data density of target
class. The support vector data description method does not
need a priori knowledge of the target class to learn the accep-
tance region, which can update easily and rapidly in real time.
Base on the support vector data description with Gaussian
kernel function, the anomaly score was deﬁned to indicate
the degree of deviation from the acceptance region, which
provides a robustness monitoring in industrial applications.
With the improvement of O&M requirements, anomaly
detection based single-classiﬁer may be more utilitarian for
two reasons in the fault early warning, which aims to reduce
the cost. First, it is easy to obtain a large amount of data
under normal operating conditions, which avoids the possible
tedious data collection in multi-classiﬁer. Second, the natural
deterioration over the entire lifecycle may make the monitored
features change, and the single-classiﬁer can be updated easily
with current normal dataset. Besides the vibration vector, many
vibration feature extraction methods have been proposed from
various aspects, the concise and useful feature is one of the
goals. There may be better solution to express the underﬁtting
of single-classiﬁer model. In addition, more early stage fault
cases are needed to validate the proposed method at the slow
rotational speeds in real production.
