Abstract--Detecting
and
classifying
gear
faults
with
limited labeled data is essential for effective fault diagnosis
in electromechanical systems. This paper presents a novel
Frequency Adaptation Graph Prototype Network (FAGPN)
for classifying various gear faults in geared motors. FAGPN
employs
customized
low-pass
and
high-pass
filters,
integrated with an attention mechanism, to enhance multi-
scale
feature
fusion
from
vibration
signals
processed
through
various
frequency-time
methods.
Additionally,
FAGPN projects embeddings onto hyperspherical space
with
a
consistency
constraint
to
improve
accuracy.
Experimental
results
on
real-world
datasets
involving
multiple gear sets in different states demonstrate the
superior performance of FAGPN. Remarkably, with a
training ratio of 1%, FAGPN achieves over 99% accuracy.
Keywords—fault diagnosis, geared induction motor, graph
neural network, limited data sample.
I. INTRODUCTION
Efficient
condition
monitoring
and
diagnosis
of
electric drives are essential for maintaining industrial
electromechanical systems [1-4]. Existing predictive and
diagnostic
methods
often
require
extensive
prior
experience and data to identify emerging issues, such as
gear faults like tooth damage and wear , and various
bearing faults (as shown in Fig. 1), in order to obtain a
comprehensive analysis [5]. This necessity arises because
data collected at different times is non-repeatable, and
characteristic values representing machine conditions can
vary within a certain range for the same model [6-8].
However, in real-world industrial settings, diagnosing
faults is challenging due to datasets collected by sensors
often
being
incomplete,
imbalanced
in
fault
classifications, and containing numerous unlabeled, noisy,
or anomalous data. Therefore, it is crucial to develop
methods that can accurately and promptly detect and
prevent potential catastrophic failures, even with limited
fault samples, to save both time and financial resources.

The most widely measured and studied fault detection
indicator is unwanted mechanical vibration, typically
caused by reciprocating forces, looseness, or resonance
[9]. By manually or adaptively extracting and analyzing
features from the time, frequency, and time-frequency
domains,
representative
and
distinguishable
fault
characteristics can be identified in data-driven diagnostic
techniques for rolling electric drives. For example, phase
demodulation
in
time-domain
analysis,
time-domain
synchronous averaging using key phase signals for
periodic
truncation,
power
spectrum
analysis
in
frequency-domain
methods,
high-resolution
refined
spectrum analysis, and cepstrum analysis for periodic
components in the frequency domain [10-12]. Although
these methods are effective, they heavily depend on
expert knowledge for feature extraction and interpretation.
To address the labor-intensive nature of traditional
feature extraction methods, deep learning techniques have
been extensively studied using vibration root mean square
values, shape factor, crest factor, kurtosis factor, and
other features. For instance, integrating a self-attention
mechanism with convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
[13], and incorporating graph neural networks (GNNs) to
effectively manage data interdependencies for multi-scale
feature fusion [14-16]. Nevertheless, these approaches
generally require large datasets for optimal performance,
which is challenging due to the scarcity of fault data
relative to normal data in practice.
To
address
the
challenge
of
small-sample
fault
classification for bearings and gearboxes, it is essential to
develop effective methods. These methods could include
improving the representativeness of features learned from
limited data or employing transfer learning techniques
[17-18]. In response to this challenge, we have developed
a GNN designed to capture data interdependencies and
dynamically aggregate multi-scale fault information from

III. EXPERIMENTS
In this section, we employ real-world benchmark
datasets with limited training samples to evaluate the
effectiveness of the proposed FAGPN. Additionally, we
present the results along with corresponding discussions
to provide a comprehensive analysis.
A.
Datasets
We focuses on a motor-gearbox system consisting of

an electrical component (the geared induction motor) and
a mechanical component (the gearbox). Uneven gear load,
irregular tooth spacing, and load fluctuations caused by
faults are reflected in vibration amplitude. The objective
is to detect and classify failures in spur gears and
planetary gears using vibration signals from multiple
sensors. Datasets from XJTUGearbox and XJTUSpurgear
were selected for the experiments [5]. Details about the
datasets are provided in Table I.
The XJTUGearbox dataset includes four planetary

four bearing failure modes: ball fault, inner race fault,
outer race fault, and a mixed fault involving the three

bearing
faults.
The
XJTUSpurgear
dataset contains four types of root cracks on the spur gear
with crack lengths of 0.2 mm, 0.4 mm, 1 mm, and 1.4
mm.


GCN uses spectral graph convolutions to aggregate
local node features effectively from neighboring
nodes by stacking multiple convolutional layers.

ChebyNet approximates spectral graph convolutions
using Chebyshev polynomials, allowing it to handle
larger neighborhoods with fewer parameters.

SGCN reduces the complexity of GCN by collapsing
multiple graph convolution layers into a single
linear transformation followed by neighborhood
aggregation.

GAT introduces attention mechanisms to assign
different weights to nodes in the neighborhood,
enabling the model to focus on more relevant nodes.

GIN distinguishes different graph structures more
effectively
than
other
GNNs
by
using
sum
aggregation followed by a multi-layer perceptron.
All models are built in a hardware environment using
Python 3.8 and the PyTorch 1.10 framework on an Intel
Xeon Platinum 8255C CPU @ 2.50GHz and an RTX
2080 Ti GPU. For each method, we ensure that the
implementations align as closely as possible with those
provided in the original papers, such as the learning rate
is set to 0.004, weight decay to 10−4, epoch to 1000 and
dropout to 0.5. Additionally, all methods use average
accuracy as the metric across 10 runs.
C.
Data preprocessing
The preprocessing steps for time-series vibration data
are as follows. Initially, the raw signals undergo Min-max
normalization
and
wavelet
decomposition
and
reconstruction for denoising, creating the feature matrix X
for network input. Subsequently, the data is transformed
using FFT to obtain the amplitude spectrum as a new
feature matrix �1 and STFT to derive the power spectral
density as another feature matrix �2 . The transformed
data is then continuously sampled using a sliding window
with a length of 1024 and no overlap.
The resulting subsamples are processed using the
RadiusGraph method, which creates connections between
nodes within a certain radius to construct graph data. Fig.
3 illustrates the process of constructing graph structures
using the RadiusGraph method. Additionally, Fig. 4
presents a flowchart detailing the entire process from data
preprocessing
to
network
training
and
the
final
classification results.

V.
CONCLUSIONS
This paper introduces a novel FAGPN for diagnosing
gear faults in geared motors, particularly under conditions
of limited data. The proposed FAGPN is designed to
effectively
learn
and
prioritize
the
most
relevant
information by utilizing a combination of multi-scale
filters,
hyperspherical
prototype
projection,
and
a
consistency constraint. FAGPN employs customized low-
