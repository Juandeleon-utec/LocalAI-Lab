A B S T R A C T  
In the present era of Industry 4.0, Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Internet of Things (IoT) are 
revitalizing the predictive maintenance systems for industrial rotating machines through real- 
time approaches. In this work, an Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) based framework is 
developed and implemented for anomaly detection in rotating machines through vibration 
monitoring. Signals are captured using a low-cost MPU6050 accelerometer with Raspberry Pi 4B 
as an edge device. The edge-centric mechanism is based on Support Vector Machine (SVM) for 
effective implementation on the edge device. A dedicated web-based platform is designed to 
communicate with the edge device for real-time decisions. The developed framework is validated 
on three rotating machines, including gear test rig, bearing test rig, and induction motor. The 
results demonstrate the robustness of the proposed method for anomaly detection on different 
machines. Also, the developed framework has the capability to remotely monitor and control the 
machines in real-time.   
1. Introduction 
Rapid automation in manufacturing industries involves a complex machinery loop system that demands an appropriate and real- 
time feedback system for effective maintenance [1]. Anomaly detection or fault detection in this regard become the most viable 
practice requiring real-time decision-making as the failure of machines could lead to catastrophic loss. The rapid development in 
information and internet-based technologies has become a boon for developing intelligent and real-time solutions for the predictive 
maintenance of rotating machines with remote monitoring [2]. The anomaly detection task has been practiced with a model-based 
approach for many years. In the model-based approach, a mathematical model and physical laws are used to simulate the physical 
properties and characteristics of the system to be monitored, such as mass, stiffness, and damping matrices [3]. However, these 
methods heavily rely on the knowledge of the machine’s internal structure and other factors, such as loading and its nature. It involves 
complex mathematical expressions, and a bunch of such complex expressions could be tedious to handle for actual industrial systems. 
Moreover, this approach is inadequate for updating real-time detection with newly observed data. In the fault detection process, 
statistical methods found their place in recent decades with the strong support of probabilistic models [4]. However, the different 
stochastic processes involved in these methods further burden the computational process.

The recent adoption of methodology in fault detection is data-driven approaches involving signal processing and AI [5]. 
Data-driven approaches in the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) assisted system are preferred over model-based and statistical ap­
proaches. These approaches provide enhanced interoperability through ontologies to provide high-level reasoning-based inquiry re­
sponses to non-expert users [6]. Among these, AI-based methods achieved universality for the fault detection task and other 
engineering applications. These methods of anomaly detection majorly involve data acquisition, feature extraction, and then classi­
fication through various AI-based methods, including Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) [7]. These methods have 
achieved excellent results in the tasks related to anomaly detection. Furthermore, DL-based algorithms are getting much attention with 
the rapid leveraging of computational power. ML-based methods, however, find effective implementation on edge devices with proper 
feature extraction due to their low consumption of computational power. However, the implementation of these algorithms on edge 
devices, along with the provision of remote monitoring to form a cost-effective fault detection system, is challenging. In the proposed 
work, an AIoT-based solution has been developed for the real-time detection of anomalies in different rotating machines by imple­
menting the machine learning model on an economical edge device and providing remote monitoring and control. The effectiveness of 
the proposed idea is validated through experiments on three different rotating machines.

2. Related work 
The above-discussed practices heavily rely on expert knowledge and primarily involve in-house monitoring [8]. Further, in harsh 
working conditions, such practices are quite challenging and need a system that can remotely work with significant reliability and 
robustness. In such a scenario, IoT establishes its presence for monitoring and real-time anomaly detection of the machines. The IoT 
communicates assertive information through wireless sensors and transmission technologies such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, WiMax, 
etc. The IoT provides innumerable advantages to humanity by creating an ecosystem providing smart work without the bounds of place 
and time [9]. IoT for condition-based maintenance becomes crucial to make the maintenance system smart enough to handle a large 
amount of data effectively and efficiently [10]. The advancement of wireless technology has made real-time data access simple by 
integrating hardware and software architecture. Researchers have done tasks to collect the data through different embedded devices 
with various sensors, followed by a flow of this data to the cloud. Four sensors were used for condition monitoring of a three-phase 
induction motor, and SVM with cloud computing was proposed. The applicability of reduced features for sending to the cloud was 
also demonstrated [11]. An Arduino-based microcontroller was used to collect and transfer the data through Wi-Fi to monitor a wind 
turbine prototype using heterogeneous sensors. Three ML methodologies were used for analysis using the local machine [12]. Another 
research work demonstrated the study related to abnormal conditions in the motor, including motor-load, uncoupling, and 
misalignment using speed and rotor position information. IEC61784–2 module-based real-time ethernet network was defined, and the 
process field network protocol that belongs to the application layer was used for information gathering. The accuracy obtained was 
quite appreciable, in the range of 87.5 %–100 % [13]. 
The industrial system structure is usually complex and involves multiple rotating machines generating massive data. The flow of 
this data to the cloud and its handling creates many issues involving its compression, security, etc. [14]. Recently, the integration of AI 
with IoT has been getting much attention for designing industrial systems that are intelligent and smart. In such a system, data 
handling is done at the edge, and the decision is sent to the cloud with further control. Such a system can be enumerated as an 
AIoT-enabled system that more intelligently examines and counter external promotion [15]. In the case of cloud-centric IoT, it is 
infeasible to process large amounts of data because of the destructive impact of the volume in the cloud [16]. Although the cloud center 
is usually equipped with massive computational capacity, such a system is ill-suited in the context of time-criticality, and applications 
require privacy-sensitiveness due to the enormous burden on the network’s bandwidth. In continuous monitoring of the rotating 
machines, a large amount of data is generated and needs to be stored for processing and analysis. So, sending this data to the cloud and 
processing that, and then making a decision could be tedious and involve the risk of triggering the fault in the meantime. 
Further, other challenges include in herent latency limitations related to network communication and the chances to disclose 
sensitive and critical information in data offloading from the cloud [17]. This critical issue can be adequately resolved through the 
integrated application of edge computing, AI, and IoT. Edge is about processing data at a faster rate and in a massive volume close to 
the generation point, providing action-driven solutions in real-time. The data is acquired through edge devices that might include some 
microcontrollers enabled with embedded sensors, single-board computers, etc. This involves data acquisition using Micro Electro 
Mechanical System (MEMS) based low-cost sensors, data preprocessing, and feature extraction to get meaningful patterns. The data 
storage at the edge ensures protection from the threat of cyber-attacks, a light access burden, and a lesser transmission delay. Further, 
this enables the user to analyze the data in case of the unavailability of the internet on the user side. Finally, the classification and 
detection of anomalies can be done through the edge devices and sending the real-time decision to the cloud, and subsequent actions 
can be made to avoid significant losses [18]. In the present work, edge-computing powered AIoT-based architecture is implemented for 
the fault detection of three rotating machines. The system is designed to make decisions at the edge, and its mutual communication 
with the web-based platform for remote monitoring. The following are the main contributions of this work.  
(1) A dedicated AIoT-based framework is designed and deployed for the real-time anomaly detection of rotating machines.  
(2) Validating the developed system on three rotating machines, including a gear test rig, bearing test rig, and induction motor.  
(3) Real-time anomaly detection and health monitoring through the developed AIoT-based system.

3. Proposed methodology 
The proposed methodology is based on the machine learning implementation on edge device and subsequent communication and 
control of different machines through a dedicated web-based AIoT platform. The complete flow diagram of the AIoT-based meth­
odology is shown in Fig. 1. 
A total of four prevalent classification models are initially trained and tested at the local machine, and their performance is 
evaluated. The training is done through the extracted features from the vibration data. Then, the substantially performing classifi­
cation model is deployed on the edge device for the real-time fault detection system.

3.2. Development of AIoT platform 
The AIoT platform is developed using a web development framework that eliminates the involvement of any third-party software. 
The data is stored using the database close to the edge server to predict different fault conditions. This end-edge-cloud collaborative 
approach lightens the data transmission burden. The interface development involves real-time data monitoring with the simultaneous 
values of nine statistical features (or health indicators) discussed earlier. The decision is perceived based on real-time testing through 
the deployed classification model, and the platform can be accessed from any part of the globe with the web address of the platform 
through proper login. Data acquisition continuously occurs with the simultaneous extraction of features and detection of the machine’s 
health. The real-time decision the deployed model predicts is reflected on the platform’s Graphical User Interface (GUI). The embedded 
edge device is coded to have the capability to turn off the machine whenever a faulty condition gets predicted and produce related 
status on the platform, as shown in Fig. 2. 
Although only real-time data is tested, the complete data history is simultaneously stored in the database at the edge. This data 
storage at the edge ensures protection from the threat of cyber security, a light access burden, and a lesser transmission delay. Further, 
this enables the user to analyze the data in case of the unavailability of the internet on the user side. Once the necessary repair or 
change of component occurs, an update is sent to the user at a remote place, and the machine can resume operation after proper 
verification. 
4. Hardware and data acquisition 
For data acquisition and real-time anomaly detection, Raspberry Pi 4B is used as an edge device. Several factors make it a preferred 
choice, including affordability, reasonable performance, small size, low consumption of power, and support compatibility. Technical 
specifications of it include a 64-bit quad-core processor with Broadcom BCM2711, quad-core Cortex-A72 (ARM v8), 4 GB RAM, and a 
dual-band 2.4/5.0 GHz wireless network. Furthermore, the MPU6050 accelerometer is used for the acquisition of vibration data. The 
leading factors in adopting this MEMS-based accelerometer include its significantly lesser cost, low power consumption, and 
compatibility with digital interfacing. The operating voltage range of MPU6050 is 2.3–3.4 V, with a maximum consumption of 3.9 mA. 
It has a vibration measuring range from ±2 g to ±16 g with a precise calibration tolerance of 3%. Here, ±4 g is selected as per the 
application related to rotating machines. MPU6050 is based on I2C interfacing, and the operating temperature range is −40 to 85 ◦C. A 
robust adhesive Beeswax (Procured from Bruel & Kjaer) with a skinny layer is used to mount the MPU6050, ensuring firm contact with 
the measuring surface. The proposed AIoT-based methodology is applied to the three different experimental setups of rotating ma­
chines at the laboratory level, including the gear test rig, bearing test rig, and induction motor. A relay module is used to trip the 
machine “ON” or “OFF” from a remote place. 
4.1. Gear test rig (GTR) 
As an essential component, gear is used in each industry for the alteration of mechanical speed in a reduced or increased form. It has 
a wide application, including aircraft, windmills, and large ships. Experimentation is done on a gear test rig run by a 3-phase, 440 V, 1 
HP induction motor used as a prime mover and a single-stage bevel gearbox coupled with a flexible coupling. Variable Frequency Drive 
(VFD) is used to control the speed of the prime mover. A total of 20 teeth in the input gear that is coupled with the prime mover and 
gear ratio is 1.5:1. Here, binary conditions are considered, including healthy gear labeled as GHL and faulty gear labeled as GFG. In 
case of faulty gear, one of its teeth is worn out. The complete experimental setup for gear fault detection is shown in Fig. 3.

5. Results and discussion 
The methodology is validated on three rotating machines described in Section 3, i.e., GTR, BTR, and IM. The best-performing 
classification model is deployed on the edge device for real-time and remote-level detection among the four classification models. 
Other factors for the deployment are the amount of computational burden on the edge device and the offloading of real-time detection 
decisions on the AIoT-platform. This section follows the description and explanation of classifiers’ performance in terms of classifi­
cation accuracy for each machine. After that, the detailed results of the best-performing classifier are explained in terms of confusion 
matrices and respective six performance measures, as described earlier. Also, the feature visualization is given for a better under­
standing and unique insight into the functional capability towards fault detection. Further, the results related to overall system per­
formance in real-time through a developed AIoT-based system are presented in the subsequent part. The classification results for the 
three rotating machines are as follows.

5.3. Classification results for the IM dataset 
As described earlier, induction motors are considered a prime source of mechanical energy transfer to rotating machines, and the 
timely detection of anomalies in these machines is an urgent need. In the present case, a multi-class classification problem is considered 
for induction motors, including MHL, MBR, and MML, as described in Section 4. The feature visualization of induction motor data in 
Fig. 8 reveals the margin clarity in a significantly powerful way, with some having weak overlap in the case of healthy and misaligned 
conditions. However, in the case of misaligned conditions, the scattering is more in range compared to healthy conditions. In Table 9, 
the performance comparison shows that SVM has more, or the same performance compared to other classification models. 
The confusion matrices based on the results obtained through SVM are given in Table 10. Here, it is observed that the misclassi­
fication is significantly less at 19 Hz and 23 Hz, but at 29 Hz, the classifier is confused between healthy and misaligned conditions. This 
could be due to the mitigation of the misalignment with the dominance of vibration due to other components. Table 11 shows the 
results of performance measures, and it can be observed that at 19 Hz and 23 Hz, all have values more than 0.9. However, at 29 Hz, the 
value for all measures got a dip, but still, it is more than 0.9, except for the kappa value, which has a value lower than 0.9. 
5.4. AIoT-based monitoring and real-time anomaly detection 
The results from the classification part reveal that among the four classification models, the performance of SVM and RNN is best 
for multi-class classification. However, for binary classification, i.e., in the case of the gear dataset, all classifiers provided 100 % 
accuracy. In the case of model deployment to the edge device and real-time fault detection to the AIoT platform, SVM seems to be a 
preferred choice as it can work well with less computational burden. However, RNN can also be deployed, but the computational 
burden will be higher in that case. So, based on the results and its inherent advantages, SVM is deployed to the edge device for anomaly 
detection. For real-time detection of different fault conditions at the AIoT platform, data acquisition is started through the Raspberry 
Pi, and random tests are performed on the three rotating machines. The feature extraction and processing are done the same way as 
during the training and testing. The observations of health indicators are also simultaneously done and matched with the previously 
calculated values. In the case of GTR for three speeds, all conditions are predicted and updated correctly on the AIoT platform through 
Wi-Fi, with an average elapsed time of 0.39 s. Further, in the case of multi-class fault detection on BTR and IM, 27 random tests are 
done at all three speeds and repeated three times on each condition, as given in Table 12. These random tests are done as there is some 
misclassification in the case of 23 Hz and 29 Hz speeds. It is observed that at 19 Hz, the detection is accurate in all the conditions of BTR 
and IM, with an average elapsed time of 0.65 s and 0.59 s, respectively. 
However, in the case of BTR, at 23 Hz, one condition, and at 29 Hz, two conditions are wrongly detected, as given in Table 12.

5.5. Comparison of the proposed method with related work 
In the literature, significantly less work has been reported on the integrated approach using edge computing, AI, and the IoT. Some 
researchers have done work on edge computing-based fault detection of rotating machines. Some of them used the method of data 
visualization on some cloud platforms. This section compares the proposed work with the latest reported work based on fault con­
dition, used modality, classifier, maximum accuracy, and whether the AIoT-based approach has been adopted or not, and it is provided 
in Table 13. Tran et al. presented an intelligent method for the online monitoring of induction motor faults along with a correction 
scheme [21]. A 1-dimensional Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) was used for the detection of faults, and a maximum accuracy of 
99.84 % was achieved. An edge computing-based solution was presented by Langarica et al. for different single-phase motor faults 
using a CMS2000 condition monitoring system. CNN was used for the detection of faults, and an accuracy of 95.18 % was achieved 
[22]. Tritschler et al. presented a strategy to diagnose bearing faults in Computer Numerical Controlled (CNC) machines using a 
microcontroller and a single-board computer. Frequency spectrums of different fault conditions were sent to the cloud through a 
message queuing telemetry transport broker [23]. 
Soother et al. presented an edge-based strategy for the binary classification of bearing faults using different algorithms, including 
Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, Random Forest (RF), and SVM [24]. For the data visualization Thingspeak platform was 
used. The maximum accuracy achieved for the classification was 90 %. Wang et al. proposed a data reduction method for detecting 
bearing faults in a permanent magnet synchronous motor using vibration data and leakage flux. STM32F407 microcontroller was used 
for the data acquisition of different faults [25]. 
The proposed AIoT-based approach involves the integration of edge computing, AI, and IoT. The data is acquired and stored on the 
edge device with simultaneous processing and real-time fault detection through the deployed SVM model. The results on the three 
machines signify the robustness and reliability of the presented method for real-time anomaly detection along with remote monitoring 
and control.

7. Conclusion and future scope 
Integrating AI and the IoT is very effective for real-time remote monitoring and decision-making, ensuring effective and timely 
predictive maintenance in rotating machines. An AIoT-based approach is proposed and implemented for real-time anomaly detection 
in different rotating machines, including gear test rig, bearing test rig, and induction motor. One binary classification problem related 
to gear faults is considered, and two multi-class classification problems are related to the bearing test rig and induction motor, 
respectively. The validation is presented at three different speed conditions. Four different classification models are tested, and SVM is 
finally adopted for deployment on the edge device based on its inherent advantage in terms of accuracy and less computational 
complexity. Substantially good classification results are obtained in the case of the three machines with 100 % accuracy in the binary 
classification of gear, and in the case of a multi-class problem of bearing, the accuracy is found to be in the range of 91.02 % - 99.33 %. 
This range for the multi-class classification of the induction motor is 93.64 % - 99.92 %. Further, the rotating machines’ real-time 
anomaly detection and control are successfully demonstrated with the developed AIoT-based system. As future work, the next 
phase of the study will include an enhanced design with more controlling parameters for real-time remote monitoring and control of 
different rotating machines.
