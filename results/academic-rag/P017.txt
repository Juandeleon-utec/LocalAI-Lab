ABSTRACT Bearings are critical components in rotating machinery, and efficient fault detection is essential
for maintaining motor reliability and operational integrity. This study proposes a novel neural network
architecture for efficient bearing fault diagnosis that simultaneously captures local and global feature repre-
sentations from log-Mel spectrograms derived from wideband acoustic emission signals. The framework
combines a multiscale convolutional neural network with a Mamba-based selective state space model
to improve the discrimination of complex fault patterns while preserving low computational complexity.
To support context-aware learning, an adaptive feature fusion strategy is adopted to dynamically integrate
features from preceding layers. The proposed framework is further implemented on an embedded platform
to evaluate its suitability for edge computing. Experimental results demonstrate that the method achieves
superior accuracy and efficiency across multiple fault types and rotational speeds, outperforming existing
classification approaches. These results support the development of fully automated bearing condition
monitoring systems and underscore the potential of in-sensor computing.

I. INTRODUCTION
Bearings are pivotal components in mechanical systems,
responsible for supporting loads and facilitating smooth rota-
tional motion with minimal friction [1]. However, they are
inherently susceptible to faults that can trigger catastrophic
failures, unplanned production stoppages, and hazardous
operating conditions [2]. Consequently, the demand for accu-
rate bearing fault detection has intensified to mitigate opera-
tional risks and prevent severe failures. Increasing industrial
complexity and system scale have exposed the limitations
of manual inspection methods, which are typically labor-
intensive, subjective, and inconsistent. In response, auto-
mated diagnostic approaches that integrate sensor-based data

In the field of structural health monitoring, acoustic emis-

modality due to their nonintrusive nature and ability to reveal
characteristic fault signatures, particularly in environments
where vibration signals are masked by noise or interfer-
ence. AE signals are inherently nonstationary and consist of

Classical spectral analysis is ill-suited for nonstationary
signal processing because it neglects temporal variations.
Although the Fourier transform is widely used to map signals
from the time domain to the frequency domain, it exhibits
fundamental limitations when applied to complex AE signals
that are critical for accurate fault identification. To address
these shortcomings, time–frequency analysis techniques have
been developed to provide joint time and frequency res-
olution. Among these, the short-time Fourier transform
(STFT) applies finite-length windows to approximate local
stationarity, generating spectrograms that describe the tem-
poral evolution of frequency components [6]. For example,
Liu et al. employed STFT to produce two-dimensional
(2-D) representations from raw signals for subsequent analy-
sis [7]. Building on this approach, wavelet transforms offer
multi-resolution analysis, enabling simultaneous detection
of short-duration, high-frequency events and long-duration,
low-frequency trends. Similarly, empirical mode decom-
position (EMD) and the Hilbert–Huang transform (HHT)
adaptively decompose signals into intrinsic mode functions,
capturing nonlinear and nonstationary characteristics with-
out predefined basis functions. Li et al. proposed a fast
and adaptive EMD framework that efficiently isolates salient
fault-related features while maintaining low computational
cost [8]. Further developments, such as variational mode
decomposition (VMD), improve decomposition accuracy and
noise robustness, enhancing reliability in practical diagnostic
settings [9]. Jian et al. applied VMD to process motor-bearing
fault signals and optimized its parameters using the secretary
bird optimization algorithm, resulting in improved robustness
and decomposition precision [10].
Over the past decade, deep learning has driven sig-
nificant advances in machinery fault diagnosis. Convolu-
tional neural networks (CNNs), in particular, have proven
effective for high-accuracy bearing fault classification
by autonomously extracting discriminative features from
visual and time–frequency representations of sensor data.
Rajabioun et al. trained a CNN on 2-D signal images
from multiple sources and achieved a strong diagnostic per-
formance [11]. Hoang and Kang proposed a CNN-based
framework with information fusion for motor-current sig-
nals and validated its effectiveness using empirical testing
of real bearing fault data [12]. Wang et al. introduced a
damage dynamics CNN to enhance interpretability while
maintaining high classification accuracy [13]. Nevertheless,
conventional single-scale CNNs are limited by fixed recep-
tive fields, restricting their ability to capture hierarchical and
scale-varying fault characteristics, particularly under noisy
conditions.
To overcome these limitations, multiscale CNN architec-
tures have been increasingly adopted. By employing convo-
lutional kernels of different sizes across parallel branches,
multiscale CNNs can simultaneously extract fine-grained
local features and broader contextual information. Atrous
convolution is another common strategy that expands the
receptive field without increasing parameter count, improv-
ing robustness and representation quality under severe noise
contamination. Yin et al. proposed a multiscale graph convo-
lutional network to capture latent relationships in vibration
signals across scales [14]. Shi et al. developed an enhanced
lightweight multiscale CNN for rolling-bearing fault diag-
nosis, demonstrating improved feature discrimination and
strong anti-noise performance [15]. Integrating attention
mechanisms with multiscale frameworks further enhances
generalization by enabling dynamic emphasis on informa-
tive features while suppressing irrelevant responses. For
example, Shen and Zhang combined a multiscale CNN
with an attention mechanism to exploit inter-channel inter-
actions and capture localized dependencies [16]. Li et al.
proposed an adaptive multiscale fully convolutional network
using large-kernel convolutions and stacked residual adap-
tive blocks, achieving superior diagnosis performance under
noisy conditions [17]. Hu et al. incorporated multiple atten-
tion mechanisms into a multiscale CNN to improve learning
of critical fault features [18], while Zhao et al. integrated a
multiscale CNN with bidirectional long short-term memory
and attention mechanisms to achieve strong fault diagno-
sis performance across operating conditions [19]. Sun et al.
proposed WFFNet, a novel neural network architecture that
integrates convolutional operations, squeeze-and-excitation
(SE) attention mechanism, and a weighted feature fusion
strategy to enhance the network’s ability to capture com-
plex patterns, thereby improving feature learning capacity
and overall performance [20]. Xu et al. developed TFDDP,
an advanced architecture that leverages an encoder designed
with multiple attention mechanisms in conjunction with
residual network structures, thereby offering a more robust
solution even with limited labeled samples [21].
Although these approaches have achieved notable success
in local feature extraction, they remain limited in model-
ing long-range dependencies and global relationships. This
shortcoming has motivated the development of hybrid archi-
tectures that combine complementary modeling paradigms.
In particular, frameworks that integrate CNNs for local fea-
ture extraction with transformer-based models for long-range
dependency modeling have gained traction. Wang et al.
combined CNNs with transformers to achieve accurate and
robust bearing-fault diagnosis [22]. Chen et al. proposed a
cross-space multiscale CNN-transformer framework, demon-
strating its ability to capture both spatial and temporal fea-
tures [23]. Fang et al. designed a multiscale time–frequency
CNN–transformer with attention mechanisms for practi-
cal fault diagnosis applications [24]. Han et al. introduced
MT-ConvFormer, a hybrid CNN-transformer framework for
multitask fault diagnosis [25]. Zhang et al. combined an
adaptive multiscale CNN with self-attention to address
the limitations of single-scale representations in noisy

substantial resource consumption, limiting their practical-
ity for real-world deployment, particularly on embedded
platforms with strict power constraints. Data preprocess-
ing pipelines often impose significant computational over-
head, and transformer-based models, while powerful, remain
resource-demanding and complex.
To address these challenges, we propose a lightweight
heterogeneous neural network architecture that integrates
a multiscale CNN with a Mamba-based selective state
space model (M-SSSM) operating in parallel. By leverag-
ing the complementary strengths of these components, the
proposed framework achieves improved diagnostic accu-
racy while maintaining computational efficiency. The mul-
tiscale CNN captures discriminative local features across
multiple scales, while the M-SSSM extracts global contex-
tual information and long-range dependencies. The outputs
from both branches are dynamically combined through
an adaptive feature fusion strategy to balance local and
global representations. In addition, AE signals are prepro-
cessed using STFT and transformed into log-Mel spectro-
grams for dimensionality reduction before being fed into
the network. The resulting framework is well-suited for
deployment on embedded systems under practical operating
conditions.
The main contributions of this work are as follows.
1) We demonstrate that the proposed method consistently
achieves high classification accuracy and strong robust-
ness under noisy operating conditions.
2) We employ an efficient time–frequency analysis
pipeline that converts nonstationary AE signals into
2-D representations using STFT and log-Mel mapping,
yielding perceptually meaningful features with sub-
stantial dimensionality reduction.
3) We design a multiscale CNN architecture that captures
diverse short-range dependencies with lower memory
usage and computational complexity than conventional
backbone CNN models.
4) We propose a heterogeneous architecture that com-
bines a multiscale CNN with a customized M-SSSM
to jointly extract local and global features, achiev-
ing accuracy and efficiency relative to existing hybrid
approaches while remaining suitable for resource-
constrained devices.
5) We validate the proposed framework through imple-
mentation on an embedded system under real-world
operating conditions.

III. PROPOSED METHOD
In this section, we present a comprehensive examination
of the proposed methodology for bearing fault diagnosis,
as illustrated in Fig. 1. The framework is organized into
two primary stages, including signal processing and fault
classification using a heterogeneous neural network.
Stage 1: Raw signals are collected using an AE sensor
mounted on the bearing housing. These signals are trans-
formed into 2-D spectrograms for feature extraction in the
next stage.
Stage 2: The generated spectrograms are used as inputs
to a heterogeneous neural network architecture designed to
extract both local and global discriminative features. The
architecture integrates a multiscale CNN with a customized
M-SSSM. Outputs from the two branches are dynamically
fused to balance the contributions of local and global rep-
resentations. The fused features are then forwarded to the
classification module, which serves as the decision-making
component of the proposed framework.

B. MULTISCALE CONVOLUTIONAL NEURAL NETWORK
Our multiscale CNN architecture is designed to capture local
features from generated spectrograms at multiple spatial reso-
lutions. By employing convolutional kernels of varying sizes,
the network simultaneously extracts fine-grained details and
broader contextual patterns inherent in the spectrogram,
as shown in Fig. 3.

operations across pixel intensities. Each convolutional layer

The second convolutional layer continues hierarchical fea-

from the previous layer. It applies two kernel sizes (3×3 and
1 × 1) in parallel and concatenates their outputs along the
channel dimension to enrich the feature space.
Following concatenation, a pointwise operation is applied
to the aggregated feature maps to reduce dimensionality.
This operation compresses the channel dimension, empha-
sizing salient features while suppressing redundancy, thereby
maintaining computational efficiency without sacrificing dis-
criminative capability.
The reduced feature map is then flattened into a 1-D vector
suitable for dense processing. The flattened vector is passed
through two FC layers, each with 256 neurons. To improve
generalization and mitigate overfitting, a dropout layer is
inserted between the FC layers, randomly deactivating a
subset of neurons during training. The detailed architectural

In this study, we integrate a multiscale CNN with a cus-
tomized M-SSSM to capture both local and global fault
feature relationships. As illustrated in Fig. 4b, the selective
scan mechanism in M-SSSM dynamically generates input-
dependent parameters from the AE features, enabling adap-
tive state updating and output projection at each timestep.
This design allows the model to selectively retain informa-
tive fault transients while suppressing lengthy background
intervals. This approach provides a more suitable inductive
bias than fixed state models or dense self-attention. Conse-
quently, the M-SSSM can simultaneously achieve efficient
long-sequence modeling and enhanced sensitivity to weak
transient fault signatures. In the M-SSSM branch, the spectro-
gram is not directly flattened into a 1-D sequence of pixels.
Instead, each row of the spectrogram is treated as a token,
where each token represents the temporal evolution of a

experimental design and evaluation methodology. Compar-

assess the robustness and efficiency of the proposed approach
across multiple performance scenarios.
A. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
1) TESTBED AND PROCESSING SYSTEMS
Following prior studies [45], [46], the experimental setup
comprised two shafts, one located at the driven end and the
other at the non-driven end, connected through a gearbox
with a damping ratio of 1.52:1. Both shafts were mounted

A wideband AE sensor was mounted on the non-drive-

All experiments were conducted using a high-performance

experimental workflow integrated MATLAB and PyTorch
at different stages. MATLAB was used for raw signal pre-

3) ABLATION STUDY
To rigorously evaluate the contribution of individual compo-
nents within the proposed architecture, a series of ablation
experiments was conducted. Key modules were systemati-
cally isolated to assess their impact on overall performance.
Specifically, the M-SSSM was replaced with a vision trans-
former (ViT) to evaluate alternative global feature extraction
strategies. To examine the role of the multiscale CNN, the
M-SSSM was removed entirely. The proposed model was
also benchmarked against lightweight CNN architectures
commonly used in embedded systems. Finally, the standalone
performance of the M-SSSM was evaluated to assess its
independent efficacy.
As shown in Fig. 12, ResNet18 exhibited the fastest con-
vergence, with training loss decreasing sharply within the
first ∼150–200 steps and training accuracy approaching
1.0 early in training. The proposed multiscale CNN, multi-
scale CNN–ViT, and the proposed hybrid model converged
at comparable rates, whereas MobileNetV2 and the M-SSSM
converged more slowly. In terms of validation metrics, the
proposed multiscale CNN and hybrid model outperformed
MobileNetV2 and M-SSSM while demonstrating stronger
generalization capability than ResNet18.
To confirm consistency, all experiments were repeated
multiple times. Table 3 compares the validation accuracy
and efficiency of the evaluated architectures. The proposed
hybrid model with adaptive fusion achieved the highest
mean accuracy (97.34% ± 0.44) and the highest maxi-
mum validation accuracy (98.09%), outperforming all other
models at a reasonable computational cost. In contrast,
MobileNetV2 and the M-SSSM yielded substantially lower
mean accuracies, while ResNet18 achieved a comparable
mean accuracy to the proposed multiscale CNN but with
significantly higher complexity. From an efficiency per-
spective, the proposed models provided a favorable balance
between accuracy and computational demand. The multi-
scale CNN achieved high accuracy while reducing parameter
count by 50% relative to MobileNetV2 and 96.9% relative
to ResNet18, offering a compact architecture that outper-
forms established lightweight baselines. Notably, ResNet18
was the most computationally demanding model and pro-
vided no accuracy advantage over the tailored multiscale
design. These results highlight the importance of multiscale
representations, which deliver superior performance with
lower computational overhead. This advantage is particularly
evident in the analysis of AE signals, where repetitive degra-
dation patterns manifest across varying characteristic widths.
By effectively capturing these multi-resolution features, the
proposed approach demonstrates superior adaptability com-
pared to baseline CNNs, which are primarily optimized
for general-purpose tasks and lack the specialized capacity
to model such scale-dependent signal variations. Moreover,
adaptive fusion consistently outperformed static fusion in
both mean and maximum accuracy with nearly identical com-
puting cost, highlighting the value of data-dependent feature
weighting. The rationale is that adaptive fusion dynamically
modulates the contribution of each branch according to the
input characteristics, selectively emphasizing the most infor-
mative representation while suppressing less reliable features.
The proposed hybrid model was also markedly more efficient
than the multiscale CNN + ViT, despite the growing popular-
ity of CNN–transformer hybrids. This advantage arises from
inherent design differences, whereas ViT depends on com-
putationally intensive self-attention mechanisms and requires
the input image to be partitioned into numerous smaller
patches for processing, the M-SSSM architecture operates
directly on the image without such preprocessing overhead.

the most favorable resource profile, achieving minimal power
draw and memory usage across all evaluated input resolu-

for edge deployments subject to strict energy and memory
constraints.
V. CONCLUSION
In this study, we proposed a novel lightweight heterogeneous
architecture for efficient bearing-fault diagnosis. By inte-
grating the proposed multiscale CNN with a customized
M-SSSM, the framework outperforms recent CNN–ViT
hybrid approaches and conventional CNN baselines in both
diagnostic accuracy and computational efficiency under
practical operating conditions. In addition, the results
demonstrate that the STFT-based time–frequency analysis
employed in this work is well-suited for resource-constrained
environments.
Despite these contributions, several limitations warrant
further investigation. First, the current evaluation is based on
a relatively constrained dataset, which may not fully capture
the operating conditions encountered in large-scale industrial
environments. Expanding the dataset in both size and diver-
sity will be essential to validate the generalizability of the
proposed approach. Second, the experiments were conducted
on a single device configuration, leaving open questions
regarding device generalization, that is, whether the model
maintains comparable performance across different sensor
types, acquisition hardware, and computational platforms.
Moreover, the signal processing and inference stages are
currently implemented as separate modules rather than within
a unified, end-to-end pipeline, which may limit optimization
opportunities. The evaluation also does not include explicit
measurements of inference latency, a critical consideration
for real-time industrial applications. Addressing these limi-
tations, through larger and more diverse datasets, systematic
cross-device and cross-platform validation, and integrated
pipeline optimization, will further enhance the robustness and
practical applicability of the proposed approach in large-scale
industrial deployments.
