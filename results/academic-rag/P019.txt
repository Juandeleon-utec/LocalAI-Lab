Abstract—Predictive 
maintenance 
promises 
to 
curb 
unplanned downtime and extend asset life, yet real-world 
deployments struggle with scarce fault labels, strict privacy 
rules, and limited edge-compute budgets. This paper presents 
an end-to-end framework that combines a physics-based digital 
twin, contrastive self-supervised representation learning, and 
FedProx-style federated optimisation to deliver sub-second 
anomaly detection on low-power industrial edge devices while 
keeping raw data on-site. A 12 000-hour pilot on five induction-
motor drives achieved an F1-score of 0.92—13 percentage 
points above the best fully supervised baseline—and issued a 
single true bearing-fault alert 19 days in advance, avoiding an 
estimated 32 500 Euro in downtime. The encoder–classifier 
stack fits in 18 MB of memory, runs at 2.4 ms latency on an 
NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, and exchanges only 6.5 MB of 
encrypted weight updates every six hours. These results 
indicate that self-supervision, synthetic replay, and privacy-
preserving federation together form a scalable path toward 
industry-grade, regulation-compliant predictive maintenance. 
Keywords—predictive maintenance, self-supervised learning, 
federated learning, digital twin, edge computing, industrial IoT, 
remaining useful life 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Predictive Maintenance (PdM) has emerged as a 
cornerstone of modern industrial asset management, 
promising to minimize unscheduled downtime, extend the 
useful life of equipment, and optimize spare-parts logistics 
[1]. 
The 
transition 
from 
schedule-based 
preventive 
maintenance to data-driven PdM has been accelerated by 
three converging trends: (a) the widespread deployment of 
low-cost sensor networks, (b) the exponential growth of 
computing power at the edge and in the cloud, and (c) 
advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine 
Learning (ML) capable of extracting actionable insights from 
heterogeneous, high-frequency data streams [2, 3]. 
Despite 
demonstrable 
successes, 
existing 
PdM 
deployments frequently face three critical challenges: 
 
1. 
Scarcity of labeled fault data. Catastrophic 
failures are rare events, making supervised learning hard to 
scale [4]. 
 
2. 
Data and model silos. Privacy and 
intellectual-property constraints hamper cross-site knowledge 
transfer [5]. 
 
3. 
Concept drift. Mechanical wear, changes in 
operating regimes, and retrofitted components continuously 
shift the data distribution, degrading model accuracy over 
time [6]. 
To address these gaps, we propose a self-supervised, 
federated learning framework that leverages synthetic data 
generated by a physics-based digital twin to bootstrap 
representation learning, followed by site-specific fine-tuning 
on a minimal set of real fault trajectories. Our main 
contributions are: 
 
• 
A three-tier architecture — Edge Sensor 
Fusion 
- 
Self-Supervised 
Pre-Trainer 
- 
Federated 
Orchestrator — that can be deployed on commercially 
available edge GPUs. 
 
• 
A 
contrastive 
pre-training 
strategy 
enabling robust anomaly detection with < 1 % labeled data. 
 
• 
An 
experimental 
evaluation 
on 
an 
induction-motor 
drive 
showing 
a 
12 
% 
F1-score 
improvement over state-of-the-art baselines while preserving 
data sovereignty. 
The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: 
Section 2 reviews the theoretical foundations of AI-driven 
PdM; Section 3 surveys related work; Section 4 details the 
proposed framework; Sections 5–6 present the experimental 
setup and results; Section 7 discusses implementation 
considerations; and Section 8 concludes the paper. 
II. THEORETICAL BACKGROUND 
A. Prognostics & Health Management Workflow 
The canonical Prognostics & Health Management 
(PHM) loop comprises four stages: Data Acquisition, 
Feature Engineering, Health Assessment, and Prognostics 
[7]. In AI-centric PdM, feature engineering is progressively 
replaced by learned representations, and prognostics is cast 
as a Remaining Useful Life (RUL) regression or a 
binary/multiclass fault-prediction task.

C. Digital Twins in PdM  
A digital twin is a high-fidelity, bidirectionally linked 
virtual replica of a physical asset [12]. For PdM, twins serve 
two roles: (i) synthetic data generation for rare-fault 
scenarios and (ii) what-if analysis for maintenance strategy 
optimization. Coupled with domain randomization, twins 
can significantly enhance model robustness to out-of-
distribution events. 
D. Edge–Cloud Continuum 
Recent hardware advances (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson, Intel 
Movidius) enable on-device inference with sub-second 
latency, critical for closed-loop control. However, model 
update and long-term analytics remain cloud-centric. 
Effective PdM therefore relies on an edge–cloud continuum 
in which compute-intensive pre-training is performed in the 
cloud, whereas real-time inference and incremental updates 
occur at the edge [13]. 
III. RELATED WORK 
A. Benchmarks and Public Data Sets 
Early studies on predictive maintenance exploited the 
NASA C-MAPSS turbofan data set, demonstrating that Long 
Short-Term 
Memory 
(LSTM) 
networks 
outperform 
traditional regression when the full run-to-failure trajectories 
are available [14]. For rotating machinery, the PHM’08 
bearing data set has become a de-facto benchmark; recent 
papers show that 1D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) 
boosted with attention mechanisms can push average F1-
score above 0.90 [15]. However, both data sets contain 
synthetic or laboratory faults and thus do not fully represent 
field conditions. 
B. Model Architectures 
Tree-based 
ensembles 
(e.g., 
XGBoost) 
remain 
competitive for tabular sensor statistics [16], but deep 
learning 
now 
dominates 
sequence-level 
modelling. 
Transformer variants such as Informer and Conformer reduce 
inference latency while capturing long-range temporal 
dependencies [17]. Self-supervised contrastive frameworks 
(SimCLR-TS, TS-TCC) have recently achieved state-of-the-
art anomaly-detection accuracy with < 1 % labelled data [18]. 
Yet, most works assume a centralised data lake and ignore 
privacy constraints. 
C. Digital-Twin-Aided Learning 
Physics-based twins have been used to inject rare-fault 
trajectories that enrich training corpora for gearboxes, pumps 
and induction motors [19]. Although promising, these studies 
typically perform offline augmentation and do not close the 
loop between the twin and the live model during operation, 
leaving open questions about synchronisation and fidelity. 
D. Federated and Continual Learning 
Federated 
averaging 
has 
been 
applied 
to 
fault 
classification across multiple wind-farm turbines, reducing 
communication volume by 92 % while preserving accuracy 
[20]. 
Continual-learning 
strategies—Elastic 
Weight 
Consolidation, replay buffers—are beginning to address 
concept drift but have yet to be validated on long-horizon 
industrial deployments [21]. 
E. Gaps Identified 
        1. 
Label scarcity persists in real plants despite 
public benchmarks. 
 
2. 
Data-sovereignty 
regulations 
impede 
centralised training. 
 
3. 
Concept drift degrades models unless 
continual adaptation is in place. 
 
4. 
Edge deployment is rarely quantified in 
terms of latency and power overhead [22]. 
These gaps motivate the self-supervised, federated 
framework proposed in Section 4. 
IV. PROPOSED FRAMEWORK — INTEGRATED DESCRIPTION 
The envisioned predictive-maintenance platform couples 
a physics-based digital twin with a privacy-preserving, self-
supervised learning pipeline to deliver sub-second anomaly 
detection on the shop floor while continually improving 
global accuracy across geographically distributed plants. At 
the sensing layer, each drive–motor unit is equipped with a 
six-axis MEMS accelerometer, a Rogowski-coil current 
probe and fibre-optic temperature sensors; the signals are 
synchronised to a common 10 kHz clock by an STM32-
based acquisition board, packetised with 4-byte Protocol 
Buffers and streamed through MQTT over an industrial Wi-
Fi 6 network. Edge computation is handled by an NVIDIA 
Jetson Orin Nano (4 GB), whose CUDA cores run two real-
time CUDA kernels: a preprocessing path that applies Tukey 
windowing, orthogonal wavelet denoising and 256-point 
STFTs, and an inference path consisting of a 1-D depthwise-
separable CNN front-end followed by a 4-layer Transformer 
encoder. The combined model, quantised to INT8 with per-
channel calibration, occupies only 18 MB of VRAM and 
yields a deterministic 2.4 ms end-to-end latency at a 
measured 1.18 W [23]. 
Model training begins in the cloud, where a high-fidelity 
digital twin of the motor-drive assembly, authored in 
Modelica and exported via the FMI 2.0 co-simulation 
standard, generates 200 h of multi-regime baseline data 
together with physically plausible fault injections (e.g., 
gradual rotor unbalance, bearing pitting, stator inter-turn 
shorts). These trajectories are fed to a contrastive self-
supervised pre-trainer that maximises the InfoNCE objective 
over augmentations tailored to time-series data (temporal 
cropping, jitter, magnitude warping, frequency masking). 
Because the twin can variationally randomise material 
tolerances, load torques and ambient conditions, the learned 
encoder generalises across plants before seeing a single real-
world fault. A linear probe fine-tuned on 0.5 % of labelled 
field data achieves an F1-score of 0.87, eight percentage

Once deployed, every edge node performs five local 
SGD epochs on freshly acquired data and transmits 
encrypted parameter deltas to a cloud aggregator every six 
hours. We adopt FedProx with a proximal coefficient μ = 
0.01 to mitigate the non-IID skew that arises because

under 
distinct

and 
environmental stresses; 8-bit weight quantisation and top-k 
(k = 20 %) sparsification reduce the upstream payload to 6.5 
MB per round—well within a 5G URLLC uplink budget. To 
satisfy GDPR, each gradient slice is pre-multiplied by a 
Gaussian mechanism calibrated to ε = 3 differential-privacy 
protection, which empirical tests show degrades macro-F1 
by less than 0.3 pp. The aggregator executes secure 
aggregation so that individual updates are never decrypted, 
and the averaged global model is redistributed to all sites, 
where a version-tagged swap ensures roll-back capability 
[24]. 
Concept drift is handled locally through confidence-
triggered pseudo-labelling: if the softmax entropy of 
successive predictions exceeds a learned threshold τ = 0.8 
for more than 60 s, the edge device initiates a replay cycle 
that mixes 20 % synthetic twin samples (representing 
previously seen distributions) with 80 % recent sensor 
windows, then performs one additional epoch of contrastive-
plus-classification 
fine-tuning. 
This 
mechanism 
cuts 
catastrophic forgetting by 60 % relative to naïve fine-tuning 
in a six-month simulated ageing study. For reliability, the 
edge software stack is containerised with balenaOS, 
enabling over-the-air updates and watchdog recovery; if 
back-haul connectivity is lost, the node continues standalone 
inference and queues updates until the next federated round 
is reachable [25]. 
From an operational perspective, the system exposes 
REST and OPC UA endpoints that feed maintenance-
planning software (e.g., SAP PM) with a continuously 
updated Remaining Useful Life estimate and a binary health 
flag. A pilot installation on ten 7.5 kW induction-motor 
drives accumulated 12 000 h of runtime; during this period 
the framework predicted the only real bearing failure 19 
days in advance, preventing an estimated €32 500 in 
unplanned downtime, while incurring an average energy 
overhead of 1.4 Wh per day per edge node. Scalability tests 
with 20 federated clients showed near-linear convergence 
speed and no significant communication bottlenecks, 
confirming the viability of rolling out to fleet-scale 
deployments. Overall, the proposed framework effectively 
bridges the four critical gaps identified in Section 3—data 
scarcity, privacy, concept drift and edge constraints—while 
providing a clear migration path from proof-of-concept to 
full industrial integration. 
V. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 
A. 
Laboratory Testbed  
Experiments were conducted on a modular rig 
comprising a 7.5 kW four-pole induction motor coupled to 
an ABB ACS880 frequency converter. Mechanical load 
torque is applied by a magnetic powder brake controlled via 
CAN-Open, enabling reproducible duty cycles from 0 % to 
150 % of nominal load. Each drive is instrumented with a 
six-axis MEMS accelerometer (±16 g, 10 kHz), a Rogowski-
coil phase-current probe (0–400 A, 200 kHz bandwidth) and 
fibre-optic Pt100 temperature sensors embedded in the stator 
windings. The analogue streams are synchronised to a 
common 10 kHz clock by a custom STM32H7 acquisition 
board that timestamps packets and forwards Protocol-
Buffers frames over Wi-Fi 6 to an on-site NVIDIA Jetson 
Orin Nano (4 GB). Five identical rigs were operated in 
parallel to emulate a small production fleet; total acquisition 
time reached 12 000 h. 
B. 
Digital-Twin Configuration  
A physics-based digital twin of the motor–drive system 
was implemented in Modelica (OpenModelica 1.22) and 
exported via FMI 2.0 for co-simulation on an 80-core cloud 
server. Fault scenarios—including outer-race bearing pitting, 
rotor unbalance, partial stator inter-turn shorts and air-gap 
eccentricity—were injected by parametric variation of 
stiffness, mass eccentricity and insulation resistance. The 
twin generated 200 h of multiregime “healthy” data plus 50 
h per fault type at three severity levels, yielding 800 h of 
synthetic traces used exclusively for self-supervised pre-
training and replay [26]. 
C. 
Data Acquisition and Pre-Processing  
Incoming packets are buffered for 2 s, after which a 
Tukey window (α = 0.25) is applied, followed by orthogonal 
discrete wavelet denoising (db4, level 3). A 256-point Short-
Time Fourier Transform (75 % overlap) produces 
magnitude–phase spectrograms that are min-max normalised 
and stacked with raw time-domain snippets into a three-
channel tensor [27]. Real-time augmentation uses temporal 
cropping (50–150 ms), jittering (σ = 3 % FS), magnitude 
warping (β = 0.2) and frequency masking (up to 10 bins). 
D. 
Training and Federated Protocol  
The self-supervised encoder was pre-trained for 200 
epochs on the synthetic corpus with the InfoNCE loss 
(temperature τ = 0.07, batch = 512). Fine-tuning employed a 
4-layer Transformer encoder (hidden = 128, heads = 4) plus 
a single fully connected classifier; AdamW (lr = 3 × 10⁻⁴, β 
= 0.9/0.95) optimised both contrastive and cross-entropy 
objectives in a 4:1 ratio. During deployment, each edge node 
performs five local epochs every six hours (mini-batch = 
128) and then participates in a FedProx round (μ = 0.01) 
orchestrated by a gRPC-based server. Gradients are INT8-
quantised and top-k sparsified (k = 20 %), limiting uplink 
volume to 6.5 MB per round. An optional DP-SGD noise 
mask calibrated to ε = 3 can be enabled; evaluation showed 
< 0.3 pp macro-F1 degradation with privacy enabled [28]. 
E. 
Baselines and Comparative Models  
Three baselines were implemented for comparison: (i) a 
fully supervised 1-D CNN trained from scratch on the same 
field labels; (ii) an XGBoost ensemble fed with handcrafted 
statistical features (RMS, kurtosis, crest factor, harmonics 
1–10); and (iii) a centralised Transformer identical to the 
proposed model but trained on aggregated raw data (not 
privacy compliant). Hyper-parameters were tuned via 
Optuna (30 trials) to ensure strong reference points [29]. 
F. 
Evaluation Metrics  
For binary health classification the study reports 
Precision, Recall, F1-score and the area under the Precision–
Recall Curve (AUPRC). Remaining Useful Life (RUL)

• 
Overall accuracy.  
On the held-out bearing-fault test set the proposed 
federated self-supervised model achieves an F1-score of 
0.92, surpassing even the privacy-unconstrained centralised 
Transformer (0.90) and outperforming the best fully 
supervised baselines by 13–17 percentage points. Precision 
and AUPRC follow the same ordering (0.94 and 0.95 
respectively for the proposed model). 
 
• 
Convergence speed.  
The learning-curve plot shows that within the first ten 
communication rounds the federated model already exceeds 
0.85 F1, whereas the CNN baseline inches up to only 0.77 
after twenty rounds. This confirms that contrastive pre-
training plus FedProx aggregation accelerates adaptation 
under non-IID workloads. 
 
• 
Remaining-Useful-Life precision.  
For the induction-motor ageing study the root-mean-
square RUL error is 27 hours for the proposed framework 
versus 41 h (centralised) and 66 h (XGBoost). The NASA 
asymmetric score drops by 48 % relative to the best 
baseline, indicating fewer “late” warnings. 
 
• 
Robustness to concept drift.  
Under two induced load-profile shifts and a voltage-
imbalance event the average forgetting rate remains below 
2.8 %, compared with 7–11 % for replay-free fine-tuning. 
 
• 
Edge footprint.  
Deployed on a Jetson Orin Nano the full pipeline 
sustains 2.4 ms inference latency at 1.18 W, leaving more 
than 60 % headroom on the quad-core Cortex-A78 CPUs. 
Quantised, sparsified weight updates limit each six-hour 
federated round to 6.5 MB. 
 
• 
Economic impact.  
During the 12 000-hour pilot the system issued the only 
true bearing-fault alert 19 days before failure, preventing an 
estimated €32 500 in downtime. Mean time between failures 
improved by 14 % relative to historical records. 
Collectively, the results validate that self-supervised 
contrastive pre-training, physics-guided synthetic replay and 
FedProx orchestration provide substantial accuracy gains 
while satisfying stringent edge-compute and privacy 
constraints. Section 7 discusses the implications and 
remaining limitations. 
VII. DISCUSSION 
The experimental results demonstrate that combining 
contrastive self-supervised pre-training with federated fine-
tuning delivers a substantial boost in fault-classification 
accuracy while satisfying industrial constraints on latency, 
power and data privacy. The 0.92 F1-score achieved by the 
proposed framework represents a practical threshold at 
which predictive maintenance begins to translate directly 
into measurable economic value: the 19-day early warning 
on the pilot bearing failure prevented more than €32 000 in 
downtime, a figure that easily offsets the marginal cost of 
the Jetson edge node and cloud orchestration. 
A key driver of this gain is the physics-based digital 
twin, which generates rare-fault trajectories that would 
otherwise be unavailable for representation learning. By 
supplying a steady stream of domain-consistent synthetic 
data, the twin mitigates label scarcity and reduces the 
reliance 
on 
costly, 
time-consuming 
run-to-failure 
experiments. At the same time, federated learning preserves 
site confidentiality: encrypted, differentially private weight 
updates allow geographically dispersed plants to share 
knowledge without exposing raw signals that could reveal 
proprietary processes or violate GDPR.

The study also highlights several limitations. First, 
although the twin captures key electromechanical dynamics, 
it inevitably simplifies secondary effects such as lubricant 
degradation and thermal cycling of cable terminations; 
future work should couple the motor model with a more 
detailed drivetrain, gearbox and power-electronics stack. 
Second, the evaluation period (12 000 h) is sufficient for one 
real bearing failure but remains short relative to the full life 
cycle of heavy industrial assets. Long-horizon deployments 
will be needed to validate the low forgetting rate under 
multiple maintenance and retrofit events. Third, while INT8 
quantisation 
and 
sparsification 
keep 
communication 
overhead modest, the current implementation still relies on a 
5G URLLC backbone; investigating ultra-low-bandwidth 
codecs or opportunistic Wi-Fi offloading would further 
expand applicability in remote facilities. 
Finally, the edge footprint of 1.18 W leaves comfortable 
thermal headroom, yet integrating the inference engine into 
existing PLC hardware would eliminate a standalone SBC 
and streamline certification. Ongoing work therefore targets 
portable ONNX runtimes optimised for ARM Cortex-A and 
Intel x86 PLCs, along with on-device continual-learning 
extensions 
that 
adapt 
the 
encoder 
without 
cloud 
connectivity. 
Overall, the combination of self-supervision, synthetic 
replay and privacy-preserving federation appears to offer a 
robust path toward scalable, maintainable AI-driven 
predictive 
maintenance 
in 
real-world 
industrial 
environments. 
VIII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 
This study has presented a privacy-preserving predictive-
maintenance framework that fuses a physics-based digital 
twin, contrastive self-supervised representation learning and 
FedProx-style federated optimisation. Deployed on low-
power edge hardware, the system achieved an F1-score of 
0.92 for bearing-fault detection—13 pp above the best fully 
supervised baseline—while holding end-to-end inference 
latency to 2.4 ms and upstream bandwidth to 6.5 MB per 
six-hour round. A 12 000-hour pilot across five motor–drive 
rigs issued a single true fault alert 19 days in advance, 
avoiding an estimated €32 500 in downtime and proving the 
economic viability of the approach. 
Future work will extend the digital twin to include 
gearbox, 
inverter 
and 
lubricant-degradation 
physics, 
enabling richer synthetic replay and multi-fault diagnostics. 
We also plan to port the INT8 model to industrial PLC 
platforms, investigate ultra-low-bandwidth codec schemes 
for sites with constrained connectivity, and explore on-
device continual learning that eliminates reliance on cloud 
orchestration. Finally, a multi-year deployment across 
twenty-plus production assets will be undertaken to validate 
long-term drift resistance, MTBF uplift and total cost of 
ownership. 
The results so far suggest that self-supervised, federated 
AI offers a scalable, regulation-compliant pathway to next-
generation 
predictive 
maintenance 
in 
heavy-industry 
settings.
