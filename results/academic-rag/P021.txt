A B S T R A C T
Intelligent fault diagnosis of rotating machinery under variable-speed and high-noise conditions remains chal-
lenge. Current deep learning-based approaches to multimodal fault diagnosis (e.g., using vibration and acoustic 
signals) face two major limitations. First, the approaches struggle to effectively fuse heterogeneous yet com-
plementary information across different modalities. Second, when employing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), 
they often rely on manually defined or static graph structures. This reliance prevents end-to-end, data-driven 
optimization and limits the adaptability to varying working conditions. To address these limitations, this paper 
proposes a novel CNN-enhanced Dynamic Graph Convolutional Network (CDGCN) for cross-modal fault diag-
nosis using vibration and acoustic signals. The CDGCN introduces an adaptively dynamic, data-driven graph 
construction strategy. A shared CNN first extracts local discriminative features from raw signals and maps them 
into probability distributions, from which Sinkhorn-approximated Wasserstein distances are computed to 
construct a dynamic adjacency matrix. The resulting graph structure enables a Graph Convolutional Network 
(GCN) to adaptively model inter-segment and cross-modal interactions, with the original CNN features serving as 
rich node attributes. This architecture effectively integrates temporal pattern extraction and spatial relationship 
learning, significantly enhancing diagnostic robustness under complex, variable-speed operating conditions. 
Extensive experiments on a self-developed multi-sensor bearing dataset validate the superiority of the proposed 
method, with classification accuracy exceeding 98.7% across various rotational speeds. Ablation and sensitivity 
analyses further confirm the effectiveness of dynamic graph construction and the critical role of the entropic 
regularization parameter ε.
1. Introduction
In modern industrial systems, mechanical equipment is undergoing 
continuous upgrades in complexity and performance. With the rapid 
development of advanced manufacturing technologies, there is a 
growing emphasis on the operational stability and safety of rotating 
machinery. Rolling bearings, as essential components within these sys-
tems, play a pivotal role in supporting rotational motion and load 
transfer. Their health status has a direct impact on the overall reliability 
and safety of industrial operations. As core elements in electrome-
chanical systems, bearing failures can lead to unexpected downtime, 
substantial economic losses, and severe equipment damage. Therefore, 
achieving accurate and reliable health monitoring of bearings is of 
critical importance for ensuring safe and efficient system operation 
[1–4].
To meet the growing demand for accurate and real-time fault
diagnosis, deep learning has emerged as a powerful tool due to its ability 
to automatically extract meaningful features from raw sensor data. 
Various neural network architectures—such as convolutional neural 
networks (CNNs) [5,6], recurrent neural networks (RNNs) [7,8], 
autoencoders (AEs) [9,10], long short-term memory networks (LSTMs) 
[11,12], and transformers—have been successfully applied in machin-
ery fault diagnosis. These models outperform traditional methods by 
capturing complex temporal dependencies, spatial patterns, and struc-
tural relationships in vibration and acoustic signals, reducing reliance on 
manual feature engineering [13–15]. However, a significant practical 
challenge remains that industrial rotating machinery often operates 
under variable-speed conditions with high noise interference. Under 
such complex and non-stationary environments, the fault signatures 
captured by a single sensing modality (e.g., vibration) often become 
obscured, unreliable, and insufficient to fully characterize the machin-
ery health states. This inherent limitation of single-modality data

necessitates the fusion of complementary information from multiple 
sensing sources (e.g., vibration and acoustic) to enhance diagnostic 
robustness. Yet, despite this clear need, most existing deep learning 
models are still designed for and applied to single-modality data, lacking 
the inherent capability to effectively perform such heterogeneous

To address the multimodal fusion challenge, research efforts have 
explored fusion strategies at data, feature, and decision levels. For 
instance, Ma et al. [16] introduced a deep coupling autoencoder for 
multimodal signal fusion, while Ying et al. [17] developed a feature-
enhance fusion network for acoustic and infrared data. Wan et al. [18] 
and Fu et al. [19] proposed networks coupling multi-sensor information. 
Zhang et al. [20] and Feng et al. [21] employed tensor representation 
and multi-source heterogeneous data fusion, respectively. Despite 
promising results, a common limitation persists that these methods 
predominantly rely on handcrafted or manually designed fusion archi-
tectures. Such fixed strategies struggle to capture the complex, dynamic, 
and nonlinear relationships among heterogeneous signals that naturally 
evolve under variable-speed conditions, often leading to degraded 
robustness and adaptability in real-word applications.
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) offer a flexible and natural frame-
work for modeling structural dependencies among multi-sensor signals 
within a unified graph structure [22,23]. By operating on graph-
structured data and updating nodes through message passing [24,25], 
GNNs can learn embeddings that integrate local features and global 
information. Among GNN variants, Graph Convolutional Networks 
(GCNs) [26–28] have gained prominence for their efficiency. Recent 
studies have leveraged GNNs for multimodal diagnosis by constructing 
graphs where nodes represent sensors/modalities and edges encode 
similarities. For instance, Wang er al. [29] proposed a temporal-spatial 
graph neural network with an attention-aware module for fault diag-
nosis, constructing graphs by treating sensors as nodes, signal segments 
as attributes, selecting neighbors via cosine similarity and calculating 
edge weights with Gaussian kernel. Sun et al. [30] proposed a multi-
sensor temporal-spatial graph network fused with empirical mode 
decomposition convolution for fault diagnosis, which constructs graph 
to mine spatio-temporal correlations. Meng et al. [31] introduced a 
multi-sensor fusion based on modal analysis and graph attention 
network for bearing fault diagnosis, which constructs graph structures 
via modal analysis (fusion video-derived phase vibration and acoustic 
signals, using singular value decomposition and frequency-domain 
decomposition to extract singular value vectors as nodes). Ren et al. 
[32] presented a cross-attention-based dual-branch graph neural 
network for multi-sensor fault diagnosis, which constructs graphs by 
taking sensors as nodes, judging edge existence through Pearson corre-
lation coefficient and calculating edge weights via Gaussian kernel 
weight function. Li et al. [33] proposed an adversarially regularized 
graph wavelet autoencoder for unsupervised mechanical fault diagnosis 
by transforming multi-sensor signal into graph-structured representa-
tions and using spectral graph wavelet convolutions with adversarial 
regularization to extract multiscale features. However, these approaches 
introduce a critical limitation that their graph structures are typically 
predefined or constructed using fixed, non-learnable similarity metrics, 
resulting in a static topology that is decoupled from the downstream 
diagnostic task.
In summary, the existing multimodal bearing fault diagnosis 
methods are subject to three significant limitations:
1) Static or fixed multimodal fusion schemes: Many current methods, 
while capable of automatic feature extraction, adopt static fusion 
mechanisms—such as simple concatenation, weighted averaging, or 
pre-defined weights—that remain unchanged across different oper-
ating conditions. These strategies lack the adaptability to capture the 
evolving and context-dependent relationships between heteroge-
neous signals (e.g., vibration and acoustic), which often leads to
suboptimal feature integration and reduced robustness under dy-
namic working conditions.
2) Handcrafted graph construction in GNNs: Although GNNs offers a 
structed way to model interactions among multimodal data, most 
GNN-based diagnostic models rely on predefined graph topologies. 
These are typically constructed using prior knowledge (e.g., sensor 
physical layout) or simple similarity metrics (e.g., cosine or corre-
lation coefficients). Such graphs remain static throughout training 
and cannot adaptively reflect the latent, data-driven relationships 
between signal segments or across modalities, thereby limiting the 
ability to generalize under varying working conditions.
3) Limited data-driven dynamic interaction modeling: While some 
recent studies have explored adaptive fusion, there remains a scar-
city of methods that fully integrate feature learning with dynamic 
structure learning in an end-to-end trainable framework. Most 
existing pipelines decouple representation extraction from relation 
modeling, which hinders the joint optimization of multimodal fea-
tures and their interactive patterns, ultimately constraining diag-
nostic accuracy and system adaptability in real industrial 
environments.
This manual graph construction hinders fully data-driven, end-to-
end training and reduces the adaptability and generalization capability 
of the models. To address the limitations, this paper proposed a novel 
CNN-enhanced Dynamic Graph Convolution Network (CDGCN) frame-
work for multimodal fault diagnosis under variable-speed conditions. 
The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
1) A novel CNN-enhanced Dynamic Graph Convolutional Network 
(CDGCN) is proposed for multimodal fault diagnosis under variable-
speed conditions. The framework jointly optimizes a shared CNN-
based feature extractor and a dynamic graph convolutional 
network, where the graph topology is constructed in a fully data-

distribution distance between node-level features, to model the latent 
interactions among cross-modal multi-sensor nodes, as shown in Fig. 1.

To ensure that the dynamically constructed graph accurately reflects 
the underlying relationships among signal segments, three guiding 
principles are adopted during the graph construction process. These
guidelines are designed to optimize node connectivity and enhance the 
representational power of the resulting graph structure.
(1) Node connection criterion: For each node in one modality (vi-
bration or acoustic), the Sinkhorn distance between the node and 
the other four nodes. The connections between nodes are driven 
by the smallest distance, ensuring that each node is connected 
bidirectionally with the node most similar to it, improving the 
flow of information between nodes.
(2) Representative node selection: After establishing the initial 
bidirectionally connections, representative node in one domain is 
selected by calculating the number of edges each node is con-
nected to. The representative node is the one with the most 
connections, effectively representing the main feature of its 
domain. This criterion ensures that key nodes in each modality 
are effectively represented, thereby improving the efficiency in 
handling multimodal data.
(3) Cross-modal connection: Finally, representative nodes of two 
modalities are connected, ensuring the strongest connection be-
tween vibration and acoustic modalities. This allows for more 
efficient information transfer between nodes of different modal-
ities, thereby enhancing the ability to learn across modalities 
features.

3. Overview of the proposed CDGCN
To enhance fault diagnosis under cross-modal and variable condi-
tions, a novel CNN-enhance Dynamic Graph Convolutional Network 
(CDGCN) is proposed, as summarized in Table 3. As presented in Fig. 3, 
CDGCN leverages two core components, a CNN-based feature extractor 
and a GCN module with a data-driven, dynamically constructed graph 
structure. Unlike conventional multimodal approaches that employ 
static or handcrafted fusion strategies, CDGCN introduces a fundamen-
tally different paradigm in which the graph topology is not predefined 
but is adaptively inferred from the intrinsic statistical properties of the 
input multimodal signals. 
The lightweight CNN-based feature extractor is designed to learn 
deep and discriminative representations from raw cross-modal signals 
(vibration and acoustic signals), effectively capturing localized spa-
tial–temporal features across different sensing sources. To improve
computational efficiency and ensuring consistency across nodes, all 
signal segments share the same feature extractor. This parameter-
sharing strategy reduces redundant computations and ensures that all 
node features are embedded in a unified representation space, which is 
essential for stable graph construction and graph-based learning. 
The key innovation lies in the subsequent adaptive graph construc-
tion module. Instead of relying on fixed similarity measures or sensor 
locations, the features, which are extracted from font-end CNN-based 
extractor, are first transformed into probability distributions using a 
Softmax-based normalization process. These probabilistic representa-
tions are then used to compute pairwise Sinkhorn-approximated Was-
serstein distances between signal segments within the same modality. 
The Sinkhorn-approximated Wasserstein distance is chosen not only for 
its ability to measure distributional similarity in a geometrically 
meaningful way but also because it is differentiable, enabling end-to-end 
training of the entire framework. To convert the resulting distance 
matrix into a sparse yet informative topology, three guiding principles is 
introduced for dynamic construction. Specially, nodes are linked to their
intra-modal nearest neighbors under the Sinkhorn distance with bidi-

4. Experimental verification
4.1. Experimental design
The effectiveness of the proposed method is verified through a self-
built data acquisition platform (as shown in Fig. 4). The self-built plat-
form is mainly composed of a motor, speed controller, gearbox, brake, 
five microphones and five accelerometers. There is a variable rotation 
speed of the motor (1200 rpm, 1800 rpm, 2400 rpm and 3000 rpm), and 
the brake is set to the released state. There are 0.5 mm deep and 1 mm 
wide grooves in the inner race and outer ring of the bearing, and 0.5 mm 
deep and 0.5 mm wide groove in the ball. Data from five health condi-
tions of bearing is collected, including normal, inner race fault (IF), 
outer race fault (OF), rolling ball fault (BF) and hybrid fault (HF).
The sampling frequency of the microphones and accelerometers are 
all set equal to 12.8KHz. Each block remains 0.64 s and the block size is 
8,192. Meanwhile, 15 blocks are acquired and total time is 9.6 s. Total 
length for each signal is 122,880 for one bearing health condition, and 
the data collected from different modalities and nodes under the 
representative 3000 rpm working condition is shown in Fig. 5. 24,576 
length segment (20% of the total signal length) was used as the test 
signal. Train signal and test signal are both divided into segments of 
length 512 with an overlap of 256. As a result, the total number of 
samples is 386 for the training dataset and 95 for the test dataset under 
each health state and rotating speed.

5. Ablation study
5.1. Effectiveness analysis of key modules
To further evaluate the contribution of key modules in the proposed 
framework, an ablation study was conducted by selectively removing 
different components, and three methods are constructed as follows:
(1) Method 1: The proposed CDGCN model.
(2) Method 2: The model is obtained by removing CNN-based feature 
extractor in the proposed method.
(3) Method 3: The model is obtained by removing Wasserstein 
distance-based dynamic graph structure in the proposed method, 
static graph structure instead.
Each experiment was conducted five time under different rotational 
speeds, with the average accuracy and standard deviation presented in 
Table 7. Method 1 (the completely proposed model) consistently ach-

with average accuracies of 99.03 ± 0.28%, 99.08 ± 0.31%, 99.96 ± 
0.08%, and 98.74 ± 0.30% at 1200 rpm, 1800 rpm, 2400 rpm, and 3000 
rpm, respectively. Removing the CNN-based feature extractor (Method
2) led to a pronounced decline in diagnostic accuracy (e.g., 74.53 ± 
1.93% at 1200 rpm) demonstrating that the deep discriminative features 
learned by the CNN are essential for reliable fault classification. Simi-
larly, substituting the dynamic graph structure with a static topology

deviation, demonstrating superior performance and stability. In 
contrast, Method 5 exhibits noticeably lower accuracy (e.g., 76.34 ±

location-based graph structure lack adaptability under various working 
conditions. Method 6 (fully connected graph) performs still substantially

static graph), indicating that a fully connected topology, by forcing 
every node to aggregate features from all others equally, not only in-
troduces noisy, non-discriminative information but also undermines the 
beneficial structural prior based on physical proximity and may accel-

degraded diagnostic performance. Method 7 achieves slightly better 
diagnostic accuracy than Method 5 and 6, yet average accuracy (83.34 ±

limitation of cosine similarity in capturing distributional relationships 
compared to the Sinkhorn-approximated Wasserstein distance.

edge construction, achieves an average accuracy of 83.81% (± 1.83%), 
performing comparably to Method 7 but still significantly lower than 
our proposed Method 4. This presentation suggests that while Gaussian-
based similarity can capture local relationships in the feature space, it 
still falls short in capturing the nuanced distributional discrepancies 
between multimodal signal segments under variable-speed conditions, 
compared to the Wasserstein distanced-based mechanism.
These results collectively confirm that the proposed Sinkhorn-
approximated Wasserstein distance-based dynamic graph construction 
mechanism is not only more accurate but also more robust and 
condition-adaptive than the conventional and advanced graph con-
struction strategies, thereby validating the critical role in the CDGCN 
framework.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, a novel CNN-enhanced Dynamic Graph Convolutional 
Network (CDGCN) was proposed for cross-modal fault diagnosis under 
different working conditions. The framework successfully integrates a 
CNN-based feature extractor with a dynamically constructed graph 
convolutional network, where the graph topology is adaptively built 
using Sinkhorn-approximated Wasserstein distances to model inter-
segment and cross-modal dependencies.
Extensive experiments on a self-built test platform demonstrated that 
the proposed CDGCN significantly outperforms several state-of-the-art 
methods, achieving robust diagnostic accuracies above 98.5% across 
all tested rotation speeds. Importantly, computational complexity 
analysis confirmed that the CDGCN maintains high computational effi-
ciency and a practical inference time, ensuring viability for real-time 
industrial deployment. Ablation studies further confirmed the critical 
role of both the CNN-based temporal feature extractor and the
